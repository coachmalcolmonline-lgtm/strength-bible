    title: "What Is Weight Management?"
    body: |
      Weight management means controlling your bodyweight — gaining, losing, or holding steady — on purpose, with a plan.

      It comes down to **energy balance**:

      - **Eat more than you burn → gain weight**
      - **Eat less than you burn → lose weight**
      - **Eat about what you burn → stay the same**

      That's it at the simplest level. But it gets more interesting when you ask: *what kind of weight?*

      You can lose fat, lose muscle, or both. You can gain fat, gain muscle, or both. The goal in almost every case is **more muscle, less fat, or both** — not just a number on the scale.

      **Important for high school athletes:** you are still growing. Your body needs enough energy and nutrients to develop, recover, and perform. Crash diets and extreme cuts can hurt your growth, your hormones, and your performance. Do this with a coach or a doctor, not alone.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Manage Weight in Practice"
    body: |
      Three goals, three approaches.

      **1. Losing fat (while keeping muscle):**

      - **Calorie deficit:** 300–500 calories below maintenance per day. Slower is better — fast cuts lose muscle.
      - **Protein:** 1.6–2.2 g/kg per day. Higher end of the range during a cut.
      - **Lift heavy:** keep intensity high (80%+ 1RM). This signals your body to keep muscle.
      - **Cardio:** moderate, 2–4 sessions per week. Don't go crazy — excessive cardio eats into recovery.
      - **Target rate:** 0.5–1% of bodyweight per week. A 150 lb athlete loses 0.75–1.5 lb per week.

      **2. Gaining muscle (while minimizing fat):**

      - **Calorie surplus:** 200–400 calories above maintenance per day. "Lean bulk."
      - **Protein:** 1.6–2.2 g/kg per day.
      - **Lift heavy and often:** 3–5 sessions per week, progressive overload.
      - **Cardio:** maintain 1–2 sessions per week for heart health, not fat loss.
      - **Target rate:** 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight per week. A 150 lb athlete gains 0.4–0.75 lb per week.

      **3. Maintaining (recomp):**

      - Eat at maintenance.
      - Lift heavy, eat enough protein.
      - Your body slowly swaps fat for muscle. Slow, but sustainable.

      **Tracking that actually helps:**

      - **Weigh yourself daily, same time, same conditions.** Use the weekly average, not the daily number.
      - **Take measurements** — waist, chest, arms, thighs. Muscle gain and fat loss can cancel out on the scale.
      - **Progress photos** every 4 weeks, same lighting, same pose.
      - **Track performance** — are your lifts going up? That's the signal you're keeping (or gaining) muscle.

      **What NOT to do:**

      - Crash diets (sub-1,200 cal) — you lose muscle and hormones crash.
      - "Cutting" during a growth spurt — you're fighting your own body.
      - Weighing yourself multiple times a day and reacting to every fluctuation.
      - Chasing a number that ignores body composition.

  - level: 3
    title: "The Physiology of Body Composition"
    body: |
      Weight management isn't just calories. It's hormones, energy availability, and how your body partitions what you eat.

      **Energy balance is real, but not the whole story:**

      - **Calories in (CI):** total food energy. Measured in kcal. Affected by appetite, food environment, and hormonal signals.
      - **Calories out (CO):** BMR (basal metabolic rate) + TEF (thermic effect of food) + NEAT (non-exercise activity thermogenesis) + EAT (exercise activity thermogenesis).
      - **Adaptive thermogenesis:** during prolonged deficits, the body reduces BMR and NEAT to defend fat stores. This is why weight loss stalls.

      **Macronutrients:**

      - **Protein (4 kcal/g):** highest thermic effect (~20–30% of calories burned in digestion). Preserves muscle in a deficit. 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day.
      - **Carbohydrate (4 kcal/g):** primary fuel for high-intensity training. Don't fear them — but they add up fast.
      - **Fat (9 kcal/g):** essential for hormones, joint health, vitamin absorption. 0.8–1.0 g/kg/day minimum.

      **Body composition vs. bodyweight:**

      Two athletes can be 180 lb. One is 12% body fat, the other 25%. The scale tells you nothing about composition. Track:

      - Body fat % (via DEXA, calipers, or bioimpedance — with error margins)
      - Waist circumference (a cheap, reliable proxy)
      - Progress photos
      - Strength performance
      - Measurements at fixed sites

      **The danger zone for adolescent athletes:**

      - **Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S):** chronic under-eating relative to training load. Consequences: hormonal disruption, bone density loss, immune suppression, menstrual dysfunction (in females), and impaired performance.
      - **Disordered eating:** high prevalence in athletes. Warning signs — obsessive food tracking, cutting entire food groups, eating alone, anxiety around meals.
      - **Weight cuts for weight-class sports** (wrestling, rowing, etc.) should be supervised by a qualified professional.

      **Practical programming:**

      - Set a target rate and track weekly average weight, not daily.
      - Adjust calories based on 2-week trends, not 2-day fluctuations.
      - Protect protein — never let it drop below 1.6 g/kg during a cut.
      - Keep lifting heavy through the process.
      - Refeed or diet-break every 4–8 weeks (return to maintenance for 1–2 weeks).
      - If you're a growing teenager, get input from a coach, dietitian, or doctor before any significant cut.

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Nutrition & Body Composition Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, body composition is governed by energy balance plus hormonal regulation plus individual partitioning.

      **Thermodynamics vs. the model:**

      The first law of thermodynamics (energy cannot be created or destroyed) is not debated. But how the body uses and stores energy is regulated by:

      - **Insulin** — promotes fat storage and protein synthesis in the presence of nutrients.
      - **Leptin** — satiety signal from fat cells. Falls during a deficit.
      - **Ghrelin** — hunger signal. Rises during a deficit.
      - **Cortisol** — catabolic under chronic stress. Breaks down muscle.
      - **Thyroid hormones** — regulate BMR. Drop during prolonged deficits.
      - **Testosterone, estrogen, IGF-1** — anabolic. Suppressed under chronic energy restriction.

      **Nutrient partitioning:**

      When you eat, calories can go to fat storage, muscle protein synthesis, glycogen replenishment, or energy expenditure. Partitioning is influenced by:

      - Training stimulus (lifting heavy signals "keep/grow muscle")
      - Protein intake (higher protein → more to muscle, less to fat)
      - Insulin sensitivity (exercise improves it)
      - Sleep (poor sleep shifts to fat storage)
      - Stress (chronic cortisol favors storage and breakdown)

      **Research findings:**

      - **Protein meta-analyses (Morton et al. 2018):** 1.6 g/kg/day is the plateau for muscle protein synthesis. Above that provides little additional benefit.
      - **Rate of loss meta-analyses (Helms et al. 2014):** 0.5–1% bodyweight per week preserves muscle and performance in trained lifters.
      - **Diet breaks (Byrne et al. 2018):** refeeds improve adherence and reduce adaptive thermogenesis during prolonged deficits.
      - **Sleep and body composition (Nedeltcheva et al. 2010):** chronic short sleep shifts weight loss toward lean tissue, not fat.

      **Advanced strategies:**

      - **Calorie cycling:** higher calories on training days, lower on rest days. Total weekly calories still determine direction.
      - **Refeed days:** periodic higher-carb days during a cut. Restores leptin, glycogen, and psychological adherence.
      - **Protein timing:** 3–5 servings of 0.4 g/kg each, spaced every 3–4 hours, maximizes MPS.
      - **Creatine monohydrate:** 3–5 g/day. Well-supported for muscle, strength, and even cognitive benefits. Safe for adolescents per most sports-medicine bodies.

      **RED-S and adolescent athletes:**

      The Female Athlete Triad and now RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport) describe the cascade of consequences when energy availability drops below ~30 kcal/kg fat-free mass per day:

      - Menstrual dysfunction (females) or low testosterone (males)
      - Bone stress injuries
      - Immune suppression
      - Reduced endurance and strength
      - Mood disturbances
      - Long-term endocrine consequences

      This is why weight management for adolescent athletes must be supervised. The risks of unsupervised cutting in a growing body are not worth the theoretical performance benefit.

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Set protein at 1.6–2.2 g/kg and don't go below it.
      - Choose a rate of change (0.5–1% BW/week loss; 0.25–0.5% BW/week gain) and stick to it.
      - Track weekly average weight + performance + measurements. Adjust on 2-week trends.
      - Refeed or break the diet every 4–8 weeks.
      - Never cut during a growth spurt without medical oversight.
      - Sleep 7–9 hours. Without it, you're fighting uphill.

# Weight Management

Weight management is energy balance plus hormones plus body composition. Gaining, losing, or maintaining requires a plan, tracking, and honesty about the numbers.
