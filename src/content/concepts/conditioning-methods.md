    title: "What Is Conditioning?"
    body: |
      Conditioning means training your heart, lungs, and energy systems so you can do more work for longer, with better recovery.

      It's not just "cardio." It's not just running. Conditioning is:

      - **Endurance** — lasting through a long game or practice
      - **Recovery between efforts** — bouncing back between sprints, plays, or sets
      - **Work capacity** — doing more work in less time
      - **Heart health** — the foundation everything else sits on

      Different sports need different conditioning:

      - **Football, basketball, soccer:** repeated sprints, stops, starts — mixed energy systems
      - **Distance running, cross country:** sustained aerobic work
      - **Wrestling, rowing:** high-intensity bursts plus endurance
      - **Powerlifting, throwing:** less conditioning, but still need a base

      **If you don't train your engine, you can't express your strength.** A strong athlete who gases out in the 4th quarter isn't strong when it matters.

  - level: 2
    title: "The Conditioning Toolkit"
    body: |
      There are four main methods. Each trains a different system and fits a different goal.

      **1. Steady-State (LISS — Low-Intensity Steady-State)**

      - **What:** 30–60+ min at low-moderate intensity (Zone 2, conversational pace)
      - **Trains:** aerobic base, fat oxidation, recovery capacity
      - **Feels like:** easy jog, bike, row, walk, swim
      - **Cost:** low — you can do it almost daily
      - **Best for:** building a foundation, recovery days, general health

      **2. Tempo / Threshold**

      - **What:** 20–40 min at "comfortably hard" pace — you can speak a few words but not hold a conversation
      - **Trains:** lactate threshold, sustainable pace
      - **Feels like:** 5K to 10K race pace, hard but controlled
      - **Cost:** moderate — 1–2× per week
      - **Best for:** improving how long you can hold a hard pace

      **3. Intervals (HIIT — High-Intensity Interval Training)**

      - **What:** short hard efforts with rest between. Examples: 30 sec on / 30 sec off, 4 min on / 3 min off, 1 min on / 2 min off
      - **Trains:** glycolytic system, VO2max, work capacity
      - **Feels like:** hard, breathless, "why am I doing this"
      - **Cost:** high — 1–2× per week max
      - **Best for:** sport-specific fitness, sharpening before competition

      **4. Sprint Intervals (ATP-PC training)**

      - **What:** 5–15 sec max efforts with full recovery (3–5 min between)
      - **Trains:** max speed, explosiveness, short-burst power
      - **Feels like:** sprinting for your life, then resting completely
      - **Cost:** very high — 1–2× per week max
      - **Best for:** speed athletes, sprinters, skill-position players

      **How to combine them:**

      | Goal | Weekly Mix |
      |---|---|
      | General fitness | 2–3× LISS + 1× intervals |
      | Team sport | 1–2× LISS + 1–2× intervals + 1× sprints |
      | Endurance | 3–4× LISS + 1–2× tempo |
      | Strength athlete | 1–2× LISS + occasional interval |

      **Rule of thumb:** the harder the method, the fewer times per week.

  - level: 3
    title: "Prescribing Conditioning"
    body: |
      Effective conditioning requires precise dosing. Here's how to prescribe each method by intensity, duration, and recovery.

      **Intensity markers:**

      | Zone | % Max HR | Talk Test | Lactate | Primary Fuel |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | Zone 1 | 50–60% | Full conversation | <2 mmol/L | Fat |
      | Zone 2 | 60–70% | Full conversation, slightly breathless | 1.5–2 mmol/L | Fat + some glucose |
      | Zone 3 | 70–80% | Short sentences | 2–4 mmol/L | Mixed |
      | Zone 4 | 80–90% | A few words | 4–6 mmol/L | Glucose (glycogen) |
      | Zone 5 | 90–100% | No talking | >6 mmol/L | Glucose, PCr |

      **Method-specific prescriptions:**

      **LISS (Steady-State)**
      - Zone 2: 60–70% max HR
      - 30–60 min continuous
      - 2–4× per week
      - Weekly target: 120–180 min total

      **Tempo / Threshold**
      - Zone 3–4: 75–85% max HR
      - 20–40 min continuous, or 3–4× 8 min with 2 min rest
      - 1–2× per week
      - Do NOT do tempo after a heavy leg day

      **HIIT (Intervals)**
      - Zone 4–5: 85–95% max HR
      - Protocols:
        - **Short:** 30 sec on / 30–60 sec off × 8–12
        - **Medium:** 1 min on / 2 min off × 6–8
        - **Long:** 4 min on / 3 min off × 4–6 (VO2max classic)
      - 1–2× per week
      - Full session including warm-up: 25–40 min

      **Sprint Intervals**
      - Zone 5: max effort
      - 5–15 sec sprint + 3–5 min full rest × 4–8
      - 1–2× per week
      - Only on fresh legs — never after leg day

      **The weekly structure that works for most athletes:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Strength (heavy) |
      | Tue | Intervals or sprint work |
      | Wed | LISS (30 min easy) |
      | Thu | Strength (moderate) |
      | Fri | Tempo or LISS |
      | Sat | Sport practice or off |
      | Sun | Off / mobility |

      **Key principles:**

      1. **Don't stack hard conditioning on hard strength days.** Separate by 24+ hours.
      2. **Zone 2 is your friend.** It recovers cheap and builds the engine.
      3. **Hard sessions are the spice, not the meal.** 80% easy, 20% hard.
      4. **Progress slowly.** Add 5–10% weekly volume, no more.
      5. **Track something.** HR, pace, RPE, or time-to-complete. What gets measured gets improved.

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Conditioning Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, conditioning is about optimizing cardiac output, mitochondrial adaptation, and lactate kinetics — then integrating those adaptations with strength and skill training without interference.

      **Central vs. peripheral adaptations:**

      - **Central:** heart adaptations — increased stroke volume, cardiac output, left ventricular mass. Driven by sustained moderate-to-hard work.
      - **Peripheral:** muscle adaptations — mitochondrial density, capillary density, fat oxidation enzymes. Driven by volume and frequency.

      You need both. Central adaptations come from zone 4–5 work (hard intervals). Peripheral adaptations come from zone 2 volume.

      **Key physiological markers:**

      - **VO2max:** ceiling of aerobic ATP production. Best trained with 4×4 min at 90–95% max HR (Helgerud et al., 2007).
      - **Lactate threshold (LT2):** the highest sustainable intensity. Best trained at or slightly below threshold (tempo runs).
      - **Critical power / speed (CP/CS):** the asymptote of the power-duration curve. Time to exhaustion = W' / (P − CP). Practical meaning: above CP, you're spending finite reserves.
      - **HRV (heart rate variability):** beat-to-beat variance. Elevated HRV = parasympathetic dominance = recovered. Suppressed HRV = sympathetic = under-recovered.

      **Programming for adaptation:**

      **VO2max (central adaptation):**
      - 4×4 min at 90–95% max HR, 3 min active recovery
      - 2× per week for 6–8 weeks
      - Typical VO2max gains: 5–15%

      **Lactate threshold (peripheral):**
      - 3×10 min at LT2 (comfortably hard), 2 min rest
      - Or 20–40 min continuous tempo
      - 1–2× per week
      - Shifts LT2 to higher intensities over months

      **Aerobic base (mitochondrial density):**
      - 3–5× per week, 30–60 min Zone 2
      - Weeks to months to see adaptation
      - The foundation for everything else

      **Concurrent training interference — the practical problem:**

      Aerobic training activates AMPK, which inhibits mTORC1 (the hypertrophy/strength signal). Practical consequences:

      - Hard aerobic work immediately after strength training blunts adaptation
      - Same-session stacking reduces the strength stimulus
      - Chronic high-volume aerobic work reduces strength gains

      **How to mitigate:**

      - **Separate sessions by 6+ hours** if both on the same day
      - **Strength first** if forced to stack
      - **Keep aerobic volume moderate** during strength-focused blocks
      - **Periodize:** aerobic base in off-season, strength in-season with maintenance aerobic
      - **Use low-impact modalities** (bike, row, swim) to reduce muscle damage

      **Sport-specific conditioning demands:**

      | Sport | Dominant system | Weekly conditioning |
      |---|---|---|
      | Sprint/power | ATP-PC | 1–2 sprint sessions, minimal aerobic |
      | Team sports (football, soccer, basketball) | Mixed, repeated sprints | 2–3 sessions: 1 sprints, 1–2 intervals, 1 LISS |
      | Middle distance (800m–5K) | Glycolytic + oxidative | 4–6 sessions: tempo, intervals, long runs |
      | Endurance (10K+) | Oxidative | 5–7 sessions: mostly LISS + tempo |
      | Combat sports | Glycolytic + oxidative | 3–5 sessions: intervals, tempo, sport-specific |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Build the aerobic base first. Do not skip this.
      - Add threshold work second. Small doses go a long way.
      - Sharpen with ATP-PC work last. It's the tip of the pyramid.
      - Track HR, RPE, and pace. What gets measured gets improved.
      - Respect interference — don't stack hard aerobic and hard strength back to back.
      - Test VO2max proxies (Cooper test, 5-min all-out) every 8–12 weeks.
