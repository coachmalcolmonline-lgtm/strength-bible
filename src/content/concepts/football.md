    title: "What Does Football Demand Physically?"
    body: |
      Football is not one sport. It's a collection of positions, each with completely different physical demands.

      **The core physical qualities:**

      - **Explosive power** -- every play is 4-6 seconds of max effort
      - **Acceleration** -- 10-20m bursts, over and over
      - **Max strength** -- for linemen, breaking tackles, and holding ground
      - **Change of direction** -- cutting, juking, coverage
      - **Repeated effort capacity** -- 60-80 plays per game
      - **Body mass** -- mass moves mass; the bigger athlete usually wins the collision

      **Position matters more than sport:**

      A 320 lb offensive lineman and a 180 lb wide receiver both play football. They need completely different bodies and completely different training.

      | Position Group | Physical Priority |
      |---|---|
      | Linemen (OL, DL) | Max strength, mass, explosive power |
      | Linebackers | Strength, power, change of direction |
      | Running backs | Acceleration, power, durability |
      | Wide receivers, DBs | Speed, acceleration, change of direction |
      | Quarterbacks | Power, arm strength, durability |
      | Tight ends | Blend of strength and speed |

      **The base for everyone:** general strength. Squat, deadlift, press, clean. Everything else builds from there.

      **The rule for football:** stronger, faster, more powerful athletes win more collisions. Build those qualities and the technique follows.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Train for Football"
    body: |
      Because football positions vary so much, training must be adjusted by position group. Here's the general framework.

      **For linemen (OL, DL):**

      - Max strength focus: back squat, deadlift, front squat
      - Heavy loads (85-95%), low reps (3-5)
      - Explosive power: power clean, med ball throws, sled pushes
      - Mass building: 300-500 cal surplus, high protein
      - Conditioning: short bursts, not distance
      - Body weight target: as heavy as sustainable

      **For skill positions (WR, DB, RB):**

      - Strength base: back squat, deadlift (still important)
      - Power: power clean, trap bar jump, med ball throws
      - Speed: 10-20m sprints 2-3x per week
      - Change of direction: 5-10-5 shuttle, pro-agility, cone drills
      - Conditioning: repeated sprint ability
      - Body weight target: lean and powerful

      **For linebackers, tight ends:**

      - Blend of both worlds
      - Heavy strength work
      - Speed and COD work
      - Balanced body weight

      **For quarterbacks:**

      - Power: med ball rotational throws, throws for distance
      - Arm care: band work, scapular stability
      - Leg strength: squat, deadlift, single-leg
      - Core: rotational and anti-rotation work

      **Sample off-season week (all positions):**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Lower body + power |
      | Tue | Upper body + speed |
      | Wed | Rest / mobility |
      | Thu | Lower body + COD |
      | Fri | Upper body + conditioning |
      | Sat | Position-specific drills |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **Sample in-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Full body (low volume) |
      | Tue | Practice |
      | Wed | Recovery + mobility |
      | Thu | Full body (low volume) |
      | Fri | Practice |
      | Sat | Game |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **In-season rule:** 2 lifts per week, 45 min max. Focus on maintaining strength, not building. No soreness-inducing work.

      **The collision problem:** football is a collision sport. Training must prepare the body to absorb and deliver force. That means heavy carries, neck work, and trunk stability -- not just squats.

    title: "The Science of Football Performance"
    body: |
      Football is a collision sport with position-specific physical demands. Here's what the research says.

      **The physical profile of football positions:**

      | Position | 40yd | Vertical | Bench (225) | Squat |
      |---|---|---|---|---|
      | OL | 5.1-5.5 sec | 24-28 in | 20-30+ reps | 1.8-2.2x BW |
      | DL | 4.8-5.1 sec | 28-32 in | 20-30+ reps | 1.8-2.2x BW |
      | LB | 4.5-4.7 sec | 32-36 in | 18-25 reps | 1.6-1.9x BW |
      | RB | 4.4-4.6 sec | 34-38 in | 15-22 reps | 1.6-1.8x BW |
      | WR | 4.3-4.5 sec | 34-40 in | 10-18 reps | 1.5-1.8x BW |
      | DB | 4.3-4.5 sec | 34-40 in | 10-18 reps | 1.5-1.8x BW |
      | QB | 4.6-5.0 sec | 28-34 in | 15-20 reps | 1.5-1.8x BW |

      These are college-to-pro reference points. High school athletes will be lower, but the ranking matters -- linemen are strongest and slowest, skill positions are fastest.

      **Energy system demands:**

      Football is dominated by the ATP-PC (phosphagen) system:

      - Each play: 4-6 seconds of max effort
      - Rest between plays: 20-40 seconds
      - Rest between series: 2-5 minutes
      - Total high-intensity time: 6-12 minutes per game

      This means training must develop:
      - Max strength and power (ATP-PC)
      - Repeated-effort recovery (aerobic base supports it)
      - Work capacity for 60-80 plays

      **Injury profile and prevention:**

      Football has the highest injury rate of any high school sport. Common injuries:

      - Ankle sprains
      - Knee injuries (ACL, MCL)
      - Hamstring strains
      - Concussions
      - Shoulder injuries

      Prevention strategies:
      - **Neck strengthening:** neck harness work, isometric holds (protects against stingers and reduces concussion severity)
      - **Hamstring prevention:** Nordic curls 2x per week (reduces hamstring injury ~50%)
      - **Single-leg strength:** split squats, single-leg RDLs (reduces knee injuries)
      - **Landing mechanics:** plyometric progression
      - **Ankle stability:** single-leg balance, band work

      **Position-specific programming:**

      **Linemen:**
      - Priority: max strength, mass, explosive power
      - Key lifts: squat, deadlift, front squat, clean, bench, press
      - Accessories: carries, sled work, neck work
      - Speed: short sprints only (10-20m)
      - Conditioning: short bursts with full recovery

      **Skill positions:**
      - Priority: acceleration, speed, COD, power
      - Key lifts: squat, trap bar deadlift, power clean, RDL
      - Accessories: single-leg work, plyometrics, hip mobility
      - Speed: 10-40m sprints, 2-3x per week
      - Conditioning: repeated sprints

      **Linebackers, TEs:**
      - Blend of both
      - Heavy strength + speed + COD work

      **Quarterbacks:**
      - Power + rotational work
      - Arm care + shoulder health
      - Lower body strength

      **Programming implications:**

      - Base lifts: back squat, deadlift, front squat, press, bench
      - Power: power clean, med ball throws, trap bar jump
      - Speed: 2-3 sessions per week off-season (position-specific)
      - Accessories: neck, single-leg, hamstring, carries
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance
      - Deload every 6-8 weeks off-season, 4-6 weeks in-season

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Football Performance Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, football performance is a position-specific optimization problem with distinct injury prevention priorities.

      **The position spectrum:**

      Football positions sit on a force-velocity continuum:

      - **Force-dominant:** offensive line, defensive line -- heavy, slow, powerful
      - **Balanced:** linebackers, tight ends -- heavy enough to take on linemen, fast enough to cover
      - **Velocity-dominant:** wide receivers, defensive backs -- fast, agile, lean
      - **Hybrid:** running backs, quarterbacks -- mix of everything

      Force-velocity profiling identifies which quality needs development per athlete. Even within a position group, athletes vary widely.

      **The neck and concussion problem:**

      Football has a concussion epidemic. While helmet technology reduces risk, the athlete's neck strength is protective. Research (Collins et al., 2014) shows:

      - Higher neck strength reduces concussion risk by 5% for each additional pound of neck strength
      - Force absorbed by the neck muscles reduces acceleration of the head
      - Neck training should be 2-3x per week, progressive

      Neck training basics:
      - Neck harness flexion/extension
      - Manual resistance (partner)
      - Isometric holds (front, back, sides)
      - Shrugs, upright rows (indirect)

      **The 40-yard dash and speed development:**

      The 40-yard dash is the NFL's signature test. It's a combination of:

      - **First 10 yards:** acceleration, force production (0-10m)
      - **Middle 20 yards:** transition to top speed (10-30m)
      - **Final 10 yards:** max velocity (30-40m)

      Most athletes are weakest in the first 10 yards -- acceleration. Training focus:

      - Heavy squats and trap bar deadlifts (force base)
      - Resisted sprints (sled pushes, band sprints) for acceleration
      - Sprints at 95-100% effort, full recovery
      - Block starts for first-step quickness

      **The in-season problem:**

      Football season is 4-5 months. In-season athletes face:

      - 15-20 hours/week of practice + games
      - Collision load accumulating
      - Strength losses without maintenance work

      Research (Hoffman et al., 1990; Ronnestad et al., 2011) supports:
      - 2x per week strength training preserves 1RM and power
      - Below 1x per week: strength drops 5-15%
      - Compound lifts preserve performance best
      - Soreness-inducing work is counterproductive

      **In-season maintenance program:**

      | Lift | Frequency | Volume | Intensity |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Back squat | 1-2x/week | 2-3 sets x 3-5 | 80-85% |
      | Deadlift | 1x/week | 2-3 sets x 3-5 | 80-85% |
      | Bench/Press | 2x/week | 2-3 sets x 3-5 | 80-85% |
      | Power clean | 1x/week | 3-4 sets x 2-3 | 75-80% |

      This is the minimum effective dose. Anything more adds fatigue without benefit.

      **The body composition problem:**

      Football has a body mass paradox:

      - Bigger athletes win more collisions
      - Heavier athletes are slower and more injury-prone
      - There's a sweet spot per position

      Linemen: prioritize lean mass gain (caloric surplus, high protein, heavy lifting)
      Skill positions: prioritize power-to-weight ratio (lean, powerful)
      Linebackers/TEs: balanced

      **Advanced programming models:**

      **1. Block periodization:**
      - Block 1 (4 weeks): hypertrophy
      - Block 2 (4 weeks): max strength
      - Block 3 (3 weeks): power
      - Block 4 (2 weeks): peaking/taper

      **2. Undulating (in-season):**
      - Day 1: heavy strength
      - Day 2: explosive power
      - Weekly rotation

      **3. Conjugate method:**
      - Max effort day (heavy singles)
      - Dynamic effort day (speed work)
      - Repetition day (volume)
      - Popular in powerlifting, sometimes used for football

      **Testing benchmarks:**

      | Test | High School | College | Pro |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | 40-yard dash (skill) | 4.7-5.0 sec | 4.5-4.7 sec | 4.3-4.5 sec |
      | 40-yard dash (lineman) | 5.3-5.6 sec | 5.0-5.3 sec | 4.8-5.1 sec |
      | Vertical jump | 24-30 in | 30-36 in | 34-40 in |
      | Bench press 225 (reps) | 8-15 | 15-25 | 20-35 |
      | Squat 1RM | 1.3-1.7x BW | 1.7-2.1x BW | 1.9-2.3x BW |
      | Broad jump | 7-8 ft | 8-9 ft | 9-10 ft |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Train position-specifically. A lineman's program looks nothing like a receiver's.
      - Force-velocity profile each athlete. Identify weaknesses.
      - Neck training 2-3x per week -- concussion protection
      - Nordic curls 2x per week -- hamstring prevention
      - Single-leg work 2-3x per week -- knee stability
      - In-season: 2 lifts per week, maintenance only
      - Deload every 4-6 weeks in-season
      - Body composition tracks position
      - Sleep 8-9 hours -- the single biggest recovery lever

# Football Training

Football is a collision sport with position-specific demands. Linemen need mass and max strength; skill positions need speed and acceleration. Train accordingly, protect the neck and hamstrings.
