  - level: 1
    title: "What Is Recovery?"
    body: |
      Recovery is what happens between workouts. You don't get stronger *during* training -- you get stronger *after*, while you rest, eat, and sleep.

      Training is the signal. Recovery is where the adaptation happens.

      **Three things drive recovery:**

      1. **Sleep** -- 7-9 hours, the single biggest lever
      2. **Food** -- enough calories, enough protein, enough water
      3. **Time + stress management** -- the harder you train, the more of both you need

      **Signs you're NOT recovering:**

      - Waking up tired even after 8 hours
      - Lifts feeling heavier than they should
      - Nagging aches that don't go away
      - Mood is flat or irritable
      - Resting heart rate is elevated
      - Appetite changes (up or down)
      - Getting sick more often

      **Signs you ARE recovering:**

      - Lifts feel strong
      - You wake up before your alarm
      - Mood is stable
      - Appetite is normal
      - Motivation to train is high

      If you're showing 3+ signs of poor recovery, it's time to deload, sleep more, or eat more.

  - level: 2
    title: "The Recovery Toolkit"
    body: |
      Recovery is not one thing. It's a stack of habits. Get the basics right before adding extras.

      **The non-negotiables (in order of importance):**

      **1. Sleep (7-9 hours)**
      - The #1 recovery tool
      - Growth hormone peaks during deep sleep
      - Muscle repair happens overnight
      - If you only fix one thing, fix this

      **2. Food (enough calories, enough protein)**
      - Protein: 1.6-2.2 g/kg per day
      - Carbs: higher on training days
      - Fat: 0.8-1.0 g/kg minimum
      - Water: 2-4 liters on training days

      **3. Hydration**
      - Even 2% dehydration impairs performance
      - Drink throughout the day, not just during training
      - Add electrolytes if you sweat heavily

      **The helpers (nice to have):**

      **4. Active recovery**
      - Easy walk, bike, swim, or mobility
      - 20-30 min at low intensity
      - Increases blood flow without adding fatigue

      **5. Mobility work**
      - 10-15 min daily
      - Focus on hips, ankles, thoracic spine
      - Not a cure-all, but it helps

      **6. SMR (self-myofascial release)**
      - Foam rolling, lacrosse ball work
      - 5-10 min post-session
      - Reduces perceived soreness, improves range of motion short-term

      **7. Stress management**
      - Life stress adds to training stress
      - Meditation, walks, time with friends
      - Sleep is the foundation

      **The overrated (skip unless you have money to burn):**
      - Ice baths immediately post-strength (may blunt adaptation)
      - Compression boots (marginal)
      - Massage guns (short-term relief only)

      **Sample recovery day:**

      - Sleep 8 hours
      - Eat full meals with protein at each
      - 30 min easy walk
      - 10 min mobility
      - 5 min foam rolling
      - Off from hard training

  - level: 3
    title: "The Physiology of Recovery"
    body: |
      Recovery is a physiological process with measurable markers and mechanisms.

      **The three phases of recovery:**

      **1. Immediate (0-2 hours post-training)**
      - Replenish glycogen and fluid
      - Reduce inflammation (not eliminate -- some is needed)
      - Restore parasympathetic tone

      **2. Intermediate (2-72 hours post-training)**
      - Muscle protein synthesis elevated 24-72 hrs
      - Connective tissue repair
      - Neural recovery
      - This is when the adaptation happens

      **3. Long-term (72+ hours post-training)**
      - Supercompensation (performance above baseline)
      - Structural adaptation (tendon, bone, capillary density)
      - Requires continued adequate sleep and nutrition

      **Key markers:**

      **Objective:**
      - **HRV (heart rate variability)** -- higher = more recovered
      - **Resting heart rate (RHR)** -- elevated by 5-10 bpm = under-recovered
      - **Grip strength** -- drops during overreaching
      - **Jump height** -- drops during overreaching
      - **Sleep quality** (via wearable) -- deep sleep % matters

      **Subjective:**
      - Perceived readiness (1-10)
      - Mood state
      - Motivation to train
      - Appetite
      - Soreness

      Subjective markers often predict performance better than objective ones. Trust how you feel.

      **The adaptation window:**

      After a training session, muscle protein synthesis (MPS) is elevated for 24-72 hours. Strength adaptations follow the same timeline. This is why:

      - Training the same muscle hard on consecutive days limits adaptation
      - 48-72 hours between hard sessions for the same muscle is optimal for most athletes
      - "Muscle confusion" is not a thing -- but recovery time is

      **Autoregulation and recovery:**

      Daily readiness varies. HRV, sleep, and stress all shift how a session should go.

      - **High readiness:** push -- RPE targets, add a set
      - **Medium readiness:** hold -- same session, same weight, watch RPE
      - **Low readiness:** pull back -- reduce load 5-10%, drop a set, or swap to mobility

      This is why RPE-based training works better than fixed percentages over the long term. The program is a plan; the athlete adjusts it based on readiness.

      **Signs of under-recovery:**

      - Performance drops 3+ sessions in a row
      - RPE creeping above target for the same loads
      - Sleep quality drops
      - Motivation drops
      - Resting heart rate elevated 5+ bpm for 3+ days

      **What to do:**

      - Cut volume 30-50% for one week
      - Increase sleep by 30-60 min per night
      - Add 200-400 calories per day
      - Reduce non-essential stress
      - If it doesn't resolve in 7-10 days, take a full deload week

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Recovery Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, recovery is a complex interplay of endocrine, neural, and metabolic factors -- each measurable, and each trainable to some degree.

      **Endocrine recovery markers:**

      - **Testosterone / cortisol ratio (T:C):** a traditional marker of anabolic/catabolic balance. Drops below baseline during overreaching.
      - **Growth hormone (GH):** peaks during slow-wave sleep. Sleep deprivation blunts it.
      - **IGF-1:** mediates many of GH's anabolic effects. Drops with chronic energy deficit.
      - **Insulin:** promotes glycogen replenishment and protein synthesis. Chronically elevated = poor recovery.

      **Neural recovery markers:**

      - **HRV (heart rate variability):** the gold standard for autonomic recovery. rMSSD and HF power reflect parasympathetic activity.
      - **Heart rate recovery (HRR):** how fast HR drops after exercise. Faster = better aerobic fitness and recovery.
      - **CNS fatigue:** hard to measure directly, but reflects in grip strength, jump height, and reaction time.

      **Metabolic recovery markers:**

      - **Muscle glycogen:** depleted 20-40% per session, restored in 24-48 hrs with adequate carbs.
      - **Creatine phosphate (PCr):** restored in 3-5 min post-effort; complete restoration in hours.
      - **Blood lactate:** cleared in 30-60 min with active recovery.
      - **Muscle damage markers (CK, LDH):** peak 24-48 hrs post-session, resolved in 72+ hrs.

      **Advanced recovery modalities:**

      **Sleep optimization:**
      - Cool, dark, quiet room (65-68F / 18-20C)
      - No screens 60 min before bed
      - Consistent sleep/wake time
      - Caffeine cutoff 8+ hours before bed
      - Evidence: biggest single intervention for recovery

      **Nutrition timing:**
      - 30-60g protein within 2 hrs post-session (higher end for larger athletes)
      - 0.5-1.0 g/kg carbs within 2 hrs for glycogen restoration
      - Casein before bed for overnight MPS
      - Evidence: moderate effect, especially in calorie deficits

      **Active recovery:**
      - 20-30 min at Zone 1 (50-60% max HR)
      - Evidence: improves lactate clearance and perceived recovery
      - Better than passive rest for same-day recovery

      **Cold water immersion (CWI):**
      - 10-15 min at 50-59F / 10-15C
      - Evidence: reduces perceived soreness, but may blunt hypertrophy/strength adaptation if used immediately post-strength
      - Use after competitions or on rest days, not after strength sessions

      **Heat therapy:**
      - Sauna 15-20 min post-session
      - Evidence: modest improvements in recovery markers, some cardiovascular benefit
      - Caution in hot climates

      **Compression:**
      - 15-30 min post-session
      - Evidence: mixed, small effects on perceived soreness
      - Not a replacement for sleep and food

      **HRV-guided training:**

      Track morning HRV (via app + chest strap or ring). If HRV drops >1 SD below baseline for 2+ days:

      - Reduce training volume 20-30%
      - Skip high-intensity sessions
      - Add an extra LISS session

      If HRV is at or above baseline for 3+ days:

      - Push training intensity
      - Attempt PRs

      HRV-guided training produces equivalent or better outcomes than fixed programming in trained populations (Kiviniemi et al., 2007; Javaloyes et al., 2019).

      **Overtraining vs. overreaching:**

      - **Functional overreaching (FOR):** short-term performance drop, followed by supercompensation. Planned, useful.
      - **Non-functional overreaching (NFOR):** extended performance drop, no supercompensation. Not useful.
      - **Overtraining syndrome (OTS):** months-long performance drop, endocrine disruption, mood disturbance. Serious.

      Signs of NFOR/OTS:
      - Performance drop for 2+ weeks
      - Sleep disturbance
      - Mood disturbance
      - Loss of appetite
      - Frequent illness
      - Resting HR elevated for weeks

      **Treatment:** rest, eat more, sleep more, reduce stress. For OTS, medical supervision. Recovery can take months.

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Track HRV, RHR, sleep, and mood. Trust the data plus your gut.
      - Non-negotiable basics before expensive tools: sleep, food, water.
      - Cold immersion post-strength may blunt gains; use selectively.
      - Active recovery beats passive rest for same-day recovery.
      - Deload at the first sign of NFOR, not the third.
      - Sleep is the highest-ROI intervention. Everything else is a rounding error.
