    title: "What Does Soccer Demand Physically?"
    body: |
      Soccer is the world's most popular sport, and it's one of the most physically demanding.

      **The physical demands of soccer:**

      - **Endurance** -- a 90-minute game with no timeouts, continuous movement
      - **Repeated sprint ability** -- 1,200+ changes in movement per game, including 30-40 sprints
      - **Acceleration** -- most sprints are 10-20m, not 60m
      - **Change of direction** -- cutting, pivoting, turning, stopping
      - **Aerobic capacity** -- the engine that supports everything else
      - **Explosive power** -- headers, shots, tackles, jumps
      - **Balance and coordination** -- dribbling, shielding the ball, staying on your feet

      **The numbers:** a soccer player runs 10-13 km per game. About 8-12% of that is at high intensity. The engine matters more than in almost any other team sport.

      **The general rule:** you can't play soccer if you can't run. Build the aerobic base first, then add speed, power, and skill.

      **The base:** general strength in the weight room. Squat, deadlift, RDL, single-leg work. Everything else builds from there.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Train for Soccer"
    body: |
      **The four pillars:**

      **1. Aerobic base**
      - 30-60 min LISS 3-4x per week off-season
      - Long runs, tempo runs, cycling, rowing
      - The foundation of everything -- soccer is a running sport

      **2. Repeated sprint ability (RSA)**
      - 6-10x 20-30 sec hard / 30-60 sec easy
      - Short sprints (10-20m) with full recovery
      - Sport-specific: small-sided games, sprint-jog-sprint sequences
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **3. Strength and power**
      - Back squat, deadlift, RDL -- single-leg focus is critical
      - Single-leg work (split squats, Bulgarian split squats, single-leg RDL)
      - Power: power clean, box jumps, broad jumps
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **4. Change of direction and agility**
      - Cutting drills (5-10-5 shuttle, pro-agility)
      - Lateral movement drills
      - Sport-specific: cone dribbling, 1v1 drills
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **Sample off-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Lower body + speed |
      | Tue | Conditioning + core |
      | Wed | Rest / mobility |
      | Thu | Lower body + power |
      | Fri | Conditioning + COD |
      | Sat | Skill work / open play |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **Sample in-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Full body (low volume) |
      | Tue | Practice / game |
      | Wed | Recovery + mobility |
      | Thu | Full body (low volume) |
      | Fri | Game |
      | Sat | Game |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **In-season rule:** 2 lifts per week, 45 min max. Practice provides the conditioning, speed, and COD work. Weight room is for maintenance and injury prevention.

      **The injury problem:** soccer has a high rate of hamstring and ACL injuries. Nordic curls, single-leg work, and landing mechanics are non-negotiable.

  - level: 3
    title: "The Science of Soccer Performance"
    body: |
      Soccer is the most-studied sport in sports science. Here's the research-consensus picture.

      **The energy system profile:**

      GPS studies on soccer matches (Stølen et al., 2005; Bangsbo et al., 2006; Bradley et al., 2009) show:

      - Total distance: 10-13 km per game
      - High-intensity running (>14.4 km/h): 2-3 km per game
      - Sprinting (>25 km/h): 0.5-1 km per game
      - Sprint count: 30-40 per game
      - Average sprint distance: 10-20m
      - Work-to-rest ratio: ~1:4 to 1:6

      Soccer is aerobic-dominant with a significant anaerobic component. Aerobic capacity supports recovery between efforts; anaerobic capacity drives the sprints.

      **Physical determinants of performance:**

      - **VO2max** (aerobic capacity): 55-65 ml/kg/min in elite players. Correlates with distance covered.
      - **Repeated sprint ability (RSA):** maintains sprint quality across the game.
      - **Sprint speed (10-30m):** correlates with scoring chances and defensive recovery.
      - **Change of direction:** correlates with dribbling, defending, positioning.
      - **Lower body strength:** correlates with sprint speed and COD.
      - **Single-leg stability:** correlates with injury prevention.

      **The injury profile:**

      Soccer has a high injury rate -- especially in the lower body:
      - **Hamstring strains (~25%)** -- sprinting injuries, most common
      - **Ankle sprains (~20%)** -- cutting, landing
      - **Knee injuries (~15%, including ACL)** -- cutting, landing
      - **Groin strains (~10%)** -- kicking, cutting
      - **Calf strains (~10%)** -- sprinting

      **Evidence-based prevention:**

      - **Nordic curls:** 2-3x per week. Reduces hamstring injury ~50% (Petersen et al., 2011)
      - **FIFA 11+:** 20-min neuromuscular warm-up. Reduces all injuries ~30-40%, ACL injuries ~50%
      - **Single-leg balance:** 3x per week for ankle prevention
      - **Landing mechanics:** plyometric progression
      - **Hip strengthening:** side planks, Copenhagen planks, band work

      **Position-specific demands:**

      | Position | Physical Demands |
      |---|---|
      | Goalkeeper | Reaction, explosive jumps, lateral movement |
      | Center back | Aerial power, strength, moderate sprint |
      | Fullback | Highest sprint distance, repeated sprints |
      | Defensive mid | Endurance, positioning, moderate sprint |
      | Central mid | Highest total distance, endurance-dominant |
      | Attacking mid | Acceleration, change of direction |
      | Winger | Highest sprint speed, repeated sprints |
      | Striker | Acceleration, explosive power, aerial |

      **Programming implications:**

      - Base lifts: back squat, deadlift, RDL, single-leg work
      - Power: power clean, box jumps, broad jumps
      - Conditioning: 3-4 sessions per week off-season, mostly aerobic with intervals
      - Speed: 2-3 sessions per week off-season
      - Injury prevention: every session (Nordics, single-leg, landing)
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance
      - Deload every 6-8 weeks off-season, 4-6 weeks in-season

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Soccer Performance Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, soccer performance is a running-economy and injury-prevention problem with specific, measurable determinants.

      **The physical demands in detail:**

      Soccer GPS studies (Bradley et al., 2009; Di Salvo et al., 2007; Barros et al., 2007) quantify:

      - **Total distance:** 10-13 km per 90-min game
      - **High-intensity distance (>14.4 km/h):** 2-3 km
      - **Sprint distance (>25 km/h):** 0.5-1 km
      - **Sprint count:** 30-40 efforts
      - **Average sprint duration:** 2-4 sec
      - **Average sprint distance:** 10-20m
      - **Accelerations (>2 m/s²):** 40-60 per game
      - **Decelerations:** 30-50 per game

      **Positional differences (elite players):**

      | Position | Total Distance | High-Intensity | Sprints |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Center mid | 11-13 km | Highest | High |
      | Fullback | 10-12 km | High | Highest |
      | Winger | 10-12 km | High | Highest |
      | Striker | 9-11 km | Moderate | High |
      | Center back | 9-11 km | Moderate | Low |
      | Goalkeeper | 4-5 km | Low | Minimal |

      **Physical determinants of performance:**

      **Aerobic:**
      - VO2max of 55-65 ml/kg/min in elite players
      - Correlates with total distance covered
      - Best trained with 4x4 min intervals at 90-95% max HR

      **Anaerobic:**
      - Repeated sprint ability (RSA) -- the ability to maintain sprint speed across many efforts
      - Correlates with high-intensity running late in the game
      - Best trained with repeated sprint intervals (6-10x 20-30m with 20-30 sec rest)

      **Neuromuscular:**
      - 10-20m sprint speed
      - Change of direction ability
      - Reactive agility (response to opponent movement)
      - Jump height (for headers, goalkeeper saves)

      **Injury prevention -- the priority:**

      Soccer has one of the highest injury rates in sport. Evidence-based prevention (FIFA 11+, Petersen et al., 2011; Walden et al., 2012):

      **FIFA 11+ program:**
      - 20-min neuromuscular warm-up before every session
      - Reduces all injuries by 30-40%
      - Reduces ACL injuries by 50%
      - Reduces hamstring injuries by 20-30%

      **Key components:**
      - Nordic hamstring curls (2-3x per week, reduces hamstring injury ~50%)
      - Single-leg balance (3x per week)
      - Landing mechanics training
      - Lateral band walks
      - Hip strengthening (Copenhagen planks, side planks)
      - Core stability (planks, dead bugs)

      **Advanced programming models:**

      **1. Polarized training (off-season):**
      - 80% of conditioning at low intensity (Zone 2)
      - 20% at high intensity (intervals, sprints)
      - Avoids the moderate-intensity dead zone

      **2. Block periodization:**
      - Block 1: aerobic base (4-5 weeks)
      - Block 2: RSA and speed (3-4 weeks)
      - Block 3: sport-specific prep (2-3 weeks)
      - Block 4: in-season maintenance (weeks 1-2)

      **3. Concurrent model (in-season):**
      - 2 lifts per week, 45 min each
      - Conditioning via practice
      - Speed and COD via practice
      - Weight room is for maintenance and injury prevention

      **The "small-sided games" alternative:**

      Many elite programs use small-sided games (SSG) for conditioning:
      - 3v3, 4v4 on reduced pitch
      - High-intensity, sport-specific
      - Trains aerobic + anaerobic + skill simultaneously
      - 2-3 sessions per week

      SSG is often superior to running-based conditioning because it trains the specific movement patterns.

      **Testing benchmarks:**

      | Test | High School | College | Elite |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Yo-Yo IR1 | 16-18 | 18-20 | 20+ |
      | 10m sprint | 1.8-2.0 sec | 1.7-1.85 sec | 1.65-1.8 sec |
      | 30m sprint | 4.3-4.6 sec | 4.1-4.3 sec | 3.9-4.1 sec |
      | 5-10-5 shuttle | 4.5-4.9 sec | 4.3-4.5 sec | 4.1-4.3 sec |
      | Vertical jump | 20-24 in | 24-28 in | 28-32 in |
      | Back squat 1RM | 1.2-1.5x BW | 1.5-1.8x BW | 1.8-2.2x BW |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Train year-round; aerobic base in off-season, speed in pre-season
      - 3-4 conditioning sessions per week in off-season
      - 2-3 speed sessions per week in off-season
      - Small-sided games for sport-specific conditioning
      - Nordic curls 2-3x per week (non-negotiable)
      - FIFA 11+ warm-up before every session
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance
      - Test every 6-8 weeks, adjust based on transfer
      - Sleep 8-9 hours -- the biggest recovery lever

# Soccer Training

Soccer is a running sport. Endurance, repeated sprint ability, and change of direction drive performance. Protect the hamstrings, ankles, and knees.
