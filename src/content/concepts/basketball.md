    title: "What Does Basketball Demand Physically?"
    body: |
      Basketball looks like a skill sport. Shooting, passing, dribbling -- those are the game. But underneath them sit physical qualities that separate players who can play from players who can dominate.

      **The physical demands of basketball:**

      - **Vertical jump** -- rebounding, blocking, finishing at the rim, jump shots
      - **Acceleration** -- first step, closeouts, defensive slides
      - **Change of direction** -- cutting, stopping, pivoting
      - **Repeated sprint capacity** -- a game is 48 minutes of stop-and-go
      - **Lateral quickness** -- defense is side-to-side
      - **Landing mechanics** -- jumping 60+ times per game requires good landing

      **The energy system reality:** basketball is not aerobic or anaerobic -- it's both. Long stretches of moderate movement punctuated by explosive bursts. Training must develop both.

      **The base:** general strength in the weight room. Squat, deadlift, press, pull. Everything else builds from there.

      **The skill:** the weight room doesn't teach you to shoot. It builds the strength and power to shoot better, jump higher, and stay healthy for 30 games a season.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Train for Basketball"
    body: |
      **The four pillars:**

      **1. General strength**
      - Back squat, front squat, deadlift, RDL
      - Trap bar deadlift for power
      - Bench press, press, row, pull-up
      - Build in the off-season. Maintain in-season.

      **2. Power and vertical jump**
      - Power clean, hang clean
      - Jump squats (light load), trap bar jumps
      - Plyometrics: box jumps, depth jumps, broad jumps
      - Med ball throws (overhead, chest, rotational)
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **3. Speed and change of direction**
      - 10-20m sprints for acceleration
      - Short sprints with change of direction (5-10-5 shuttle)
      - Lateral slides and defensive slide drills
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **4. Conditioning**
      - Repeated sprint ability: 10-30 sec efforts with 30-60 sec rest
      - Sport-specific: full-court drills, sprint-jog-sprint sequences
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **Sample off-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Lower body + power (jumps) |
      | Tue | Upper body + skill work |
      | Wed | Rest / mobility |
      | Thu | Lower body + sprints |
      | Fri | Upper body + conditioning |
      | Sat | Skill work / open gym |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **Sample in-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Full body (low volume) + jumps |
      | Tue | Practice / game |
      | Wed | Recovery + skill work |
      | Thu | Full body (low volume) |
      | Fri | Game |
      | Sat | Game |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **In-season rule:** 2 lifts per week, 45 min max. Focus on maintaining strength and jump ability, not building new. No soreness-inducing work.

      **Jump training priority:** vertical jump is the single most trainable basketball quality. Every athlete should jump 2-3x per week -- off-season for volume, in-season for maintenance.

  - level: 3
    title: "The Science of Basketball Performance"
    body: |
      Basketball performance is driven by measurable physical qualities, most of which are trainable in the weight room.

      **Vertical jump -- the signature quality:**

      Vertical jump is the most important physical predictor of basketball success. It correlates with:
      - Rebounds
      - Blocks
      - Finishing at the rim
      - Contested jump shots

      Physical determinants:
      - Lower body strength (squat 1RM correlates with jump)
      - Rate of force development (RFD)
      - Stretch-shortening cycle (SSC) efficiency
      - Body composition (power-to-weight ratio)
      - Landing mechanics

      Training to improve vertical:
      - Squat 1.5-2x bodyweight (foundation)
      - Power clean or trap bar jump for explosive hip extension
      - Plyometrics: box jumps, depth jumps, broad jumps
      - Jump-specific: approach jump, standing jump, single-leg jump
      - Volume: 60-120 ground contacts per week (off-season)

      **Change of direction:**

      Basketball demands constant cutting, stopping, and pivoting. Physical determinants:
      - Hip and ankle mobility
      - Single-leg strength and stability
      - Eccentric quad control (landing)
      - Reaction time

      Training to improve COD:
      - Single-leg squats, split squats, lunges
      - Nordic curls (hamstring health)
      - Lateral band walks
      - Change-of-direction drills (5-10-5, pro-agility)
      - 2-3x per week off-season

      **Repeated sprint ability:**

      A basketball game involves ~100 high-intensity efforts. Physical determinants:
      - Aerobic base (recovery between efforts)
      - Anaerobic capacity (glycolytic system)
      - Sprint mechanics

      Training to improve RSA:
      - Repeated sprint intervals: 6-10x 20-30m with 30-45 sec rest
      - Sport-specific conditioning: full-court drills
      - Aerobic base work in the off-season

      **Position-specific demands:**

      | Position | Primary Physical Demands |
      |---|---|
      | Point guard | Acceleration, change of direction, quickness |
      | Shooting guard | Speed, jump, shooting stamina |
      | Small forward | Versatility -- mix of speed, strength, jump |
      | Power forward | Strength, jump, physical play |
      | Center | Max strength, jump, landing, body mass |

      **Programming implications:**

      - Base lifts: back squat, trap bar deadlift, RDL, press, pull-up
      - Power: power clean, trap bar jump, jump squat
      - Plyos: box jumps, depth jumps, broad jumps, lateral bounds
      - Speed: 10m sprints, change of direction
      - Conditioning: repeated sprints, sport-specific drills
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance, no soreness work
      - Jump 2-3x per week off-season, 1-2x per week in-season

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Basketball Performance Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, basketball performance is a biometric problem with measurable inputs and specific outputs.

      **Force-velocity profiling for basketball athletes:**

      The vertical jump is fundamentally a force-velocity expression. Different athletes have different profiles:

      - **Force-deficient jumpers:** strong squat, weak jump. Need more jump-specific and plyometric work.
      - **Velocity-deficient jumpers:** high jump, weak squat. Need more strength development.
      - **Balanced:** both qualities developing together. Continue current programming.

      Elite S&C programs use force plates and VBT to profile each athlete. Without equipment, you can estimate: high squat relative to jump = velocity-deficient; high jump relative to squat = force-deficient.

      **The stretch-shortening cycle (SSC) in basketball:**

      Vertical jump uses the SSC. When the athlete bends down and immediately jumps, the SSC stores and releases elastic energy.

      Two types:
      - **Slow SSC:** >250ms (countermovement jump) -- trained by strength work
      - **Fast SSC:** <250ms (depth jump, reactive jump) -- trained by plyometrics

      Basketball needs both:
      - Rebounding and blocked shots use slow SSC
      - Second-jump quickness uses fast SSC
      - Approach jumps use a blend

      **The landing problem:**

      Basketball athletes jump 60+ times per game. Each landing produces 3-6x bodyweight of force through the legs. Poor landing mechanics lead to:
      - Patellar tendinopathy (jumper's knee)
      - ACL injuries (especially in female athletes)
      - Ankle sprains
      - Low back pain

      Training to improve landing:
      - Depth drops (land and hold)
      - Box jumps with soft landing
      - Single-leg landing drills
      - Nordic curls for hamstring control
      - Ankle stability work

      **Injury prevention -- the ACL and ankle focus:**

      Female basketball athletes have 3-6x higher ACL injury rates than male athletes. Mechanisms:
      - Valgus collapse (knees caving in on landing)
      - Quad-dominant landing
      - Insufficient hamstring strength
      - Neuromuscular control deficits

      Evidence-based prevention (FIFA 11+, PEP program):
      - Neuromuscular training 2-3x per week
      - Single-leg strength work
      - Plyometrics with focus on landing
      - Balance and proprioception drills
      - Hamstring strengthening (Nordics)

      These programs reduce ACL injuries by 50-70% in female athletes.

      **Advanced programming models:**

      **1. Undulating periodization:**
      - Vary volume and intensity session to session
      - Heavy, moderate, light, moderate -- repeat weekly
      - Preserves adaptation while reducing staleness

      **2. Block periodization:**
      - Block 1 (4 weeks): accumulation -- high volume, moderate intensity
      - Block 2 (3 weeks): transmutation -- moderate volume, high intensity
      - Block 3 (2 weeks): realization -- low volume, peak intensity
      - Block 4 (1 week): deload or test

      **3. Concurrent model:**
      - Strength work + plyometrics + sprint work same session
      - Order: strength first, then plyos, then sprints
      - Conditioning last (or separate day)

      **Testing battery:**

      | Test | High School | College | Pro |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Vertical jump | 20-24 in | 24-30 in | 30-40 in |
      | Standing broad jump | 7-8 ft | 8-9 ft | 9-10 ft |
      | 10m sprint | 1.8-2.0 sec | 1.7-1.85 sec | 1.6-1.75 sec |
      | 5-10-5 shuttle | 4.8-5.2 sec | 4.4-4.8 sec | 4.2-4.6 sec |
      | Back squat 1RM | 1.2-1.5x BW | 1.5-1.8x BW | 1.8-2.2x BW |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Train year-round; emphasize different qualities by phase
      - Off-season: build strength + jump base
      - Pre-season: sharpen jump and change of direction
      - In-season: maintain with 2x per week
      - Post-season: rest 2-4 weeks, then rebuild
      - Prioritize landing mechanics -- they protect against injury
      - Never sacrifice jump quality for volume. Quality over quantity.
      - Test every 6-8 weeks. Adjust based on transfer.

# Basketball Training

Basketball is a jump-and-cut sport. Vertical jump, change of direction, and repeated sprint ability drive performance. Train them year-round, protect the ankles and knees.
