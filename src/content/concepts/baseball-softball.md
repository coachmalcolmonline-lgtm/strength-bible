    title: "What Does Baseball Demand Physically?"
    body: |
      Baseball and softball look like skill sports. And they are -- hitting, throwing, and fielding are the game.

      But the physical qualities underneath those skills are trainable, and they separate players. Two athletes with the same swing might hit the ball at different exit velocities because one is stronger and more powerful. Two fielders with the same mechanics might reach a different number of balls because one is faster.

      **The physical demands of baseball/softball:**

      - **Rotational power** -- hitting and throwing are rotational movements driven by the hips and trunk
      - **Sprint speed** -- home to first, outfield range, base stealing
      - **Arm strength and health** -- throwing velocity and durability
      - **Acceleration** -- first-step quickness on the bases and in the field
      - **Repeated effort capacity** -- a game has 100+ high-intensity movements

      **It's not one thing.** Pitchers need rotational power and shoulder health. Catchers need hip mobility and repeated squat capacity. Outfielders need sprint speed and arm strength. First basemen need less of all of it. Position matters.

      **The base:** general strength in the weight room. Squat, deadlift, press, pull. Everything else builds from there.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Train for Baseball / Softball"
    body: |
      **The four pillars:**

      **1. General strength**
      - Back squat, front squat, deadlift, RDL
      - Bench press, press, row, pull-up
      - Build the base in the off-season. Maintain in-season.

      **2. Rotational power**
      - Med ball rotational throws (wall or partner)
      - Landmine rotations
      - Cable chops and lifts
      - Medicine ball slams
      - These transfer directly to hitting and throwing

      **3. Sprint speed and acceleration**
      - 10-20m sprints for acceleration
      - 30-60m sprints for top speed
      - Resisted sprints (sled, band) for force development
      - Change of direction drills for fielding
      - 2-3 sessions per week

      **4. Arm care**
      - Band external rotations (rotator cuff)
      - Scapular work (rows, face pulls, YTW)
      - J-band routine before throwing
      - Never throw through pain

      **Sample off-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Lower body + rotational med ball |
      | Tue | Upper body + arm care + sprint work |
      | Wed | Rest |
      | Thu | Lower body + power clean |
      | Fri | Upper body + rotational work |
      | Sat | Sprint work + mobility |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **Sample in-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Full body (low volume) |
      | Tue | Game |
      | Wed | Arm care + mobility |
      | Thu | Full body (low volume) |
      | Fri | Game |
      | Sat | Game |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **In-season rule:** 2 lifts per week, 45 min max, no soreness-inducing work.

      **Pitcher-specific considerations:**

      - Shoulder health is the entire game
      - Rotator cuff and scapular work every session
      - No heavy overhead pressing on throwing days
      - Never lift hard the day before a start
      - Band work before and after throwing, every time
      - If the shoulder hurts, stop. See a professional.

  - level: 3
    title: "The Science of Baseball Performance"
    body: |
      Baseball performance is driven by measurable physical qualities, most of which are trainable.

      **Hitting -- exit velocity and bat speed:**

      - Exit velocity (how fast the ball leaves the bat) correlates strongly with bat speed and rotational power
      - Bat speed correlates with hip rotation velocity and trunk strength
      - Key physical determinants: hip rotators, obliques, thoracic mobility, forearm and grip strength

      Training implications:
      - Med ball rotational throws at high velocity
      - Landmine rotations with load
      - Cable chops
      - Heavy carries for grip
      - Squat and deadlift for base strength

      **Throwing -- velocity and health:**

      - Throwing velocity correlates with shoulder external rotation strength, scapular stability, and hip-shoulder separation
      - Elbow and shoulder injury risk increases with poor mechanics and excessive volume
      - Key physical determinants: rotator cuff strength, scapular control, core stability, hip mobility

      Training implications:
      - Band external rotations (3x15 each session)
      - Face pulls, YTW raises
      - Anti-rotation core work (Pallof press, suitcase carries)
      - Hip mobility work

      **Sprinting -- home to first and outfield range:**

      - 60-yard dash is the standard test (or 40-yard for evaluation)
      - Correlates with 10m acceleration and top-end speed
      - Key physical determinants: leg strength, posterior chain power, sprint mechanics

      Training implications:
      - Sprint work 2-3x per week in the off-season
      - Resisted sprinting for acceleration
      - Hang power clean for hip extension power
      - Nordic curls for hamstring health

      **Position-specific demands:**

      | Position | Primary Physical Demands |
      |---|---|
      | Pitcher (starting) | Shoulder health, endurance, rotational power |
      | Pitcher (relief) | Shoulder health, repeated high-intensity |
      | Catcher | Hip mobility, repeated squat capacity, arm strength |
      | First base | Minimal sprint, moderate power |
      | Second base | Acceleration, change of direction, moderate arm |
      | Shortstop | Acceleration, arm strength, quickness |
      | Third base | Arm strength, reaction time, moderate sprint |
      | Outfield | Sprint speed, arm strength, route running |

      **Programming implications:**

      - Base lifts: back squat, deadlift, press, row (1.5-2x BW squat for serious players)
      - Power: power clean, med ball throws
      - Speed: sprint work 2-3x per week off-season
      - Arm care: band work every session
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance only
      - Never max out or go to failure in-season

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Baseball Performance Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, baseball performance is a biometric problem with measurable inputs (strength, power, mobility) and specific outputs (exit velo, throwing velo, sprint times).

      **Force-velocity profiling for hitters:**

      Recent research (Driveline Baseball, others) has moved toward measurement-driven training:

      - **Bat speed sensors** measure swing velocity
      - **Blast Motion / HitTrax** measure exit velocity and launch angle
      - **Force plate swings** measure ground reaction forces during the swing

      The connection: hitters who train rotational power in the weight room show measurable increases in bat speed and exit velocity.

      **Programmatic approach:**

      1. Assess baseline (exit velo, bat speed, sprint times, arm strength)
      2. Build general strength in the off-season
      3. Add rotational power work
      4. Add sprint and change-of-direction work
      5. Reassess every 4-6 weeks
      6. Adjust based on what's improving and what's stalled

      **The velocity-based training (VBT) connection:**

      For pitchers and position players, VBT is becoming standard. Bar velocity during lifting sessions tells you how fresh the athlete is and when to push vs. back off. This is critical for in-season athletes who are already managing high throwing volume.

      **The "arm care" problem:**

      Baseball has an injury epidemic -- particularly UCL (Tommy John) injuries in pitchers. Contributing factors:

      - Year-round throwing (no off-season rest)
      - High pitch counts at young ages
      - Poor mechanics
      - Insufficient strength in rotator cuff and scapular stabilizers
      - Insufficient hip mobility (reduces force transfer, overloads arm)

      Training solutions:
      - Rotator cuff work every session
      - Scapular stability work (rows, YTWs, face pulls)
      - Hip mobility work
      - Core anti-rotation and anti-extension work
      - Full off-season rest from throwing (2-4 weeks minimum)

      **Sprint mechanics and acceleration:**

      Home-to-first time is a difference-maker. Elite runners reach first in under 4.0 seconds. The physical determinants:

      - 10m acceleration: quad and glute strength, sprint mechanics
      - Top-end speed (for outfielders): hamstring strength, hip mobility, stride length
      - Resisted sprinting transfers directly to acceleration

      **The in-season maintenance problem:**

      Research on in-season strength maintenance shows:

      - 1-2 sessions per week at 80-85% intensity maintains strength for 10-12 weeks
      - Below 1x per week: strength drops
      - Above 3x per week: performance drops due to fatigue
      - Compound lifts (squat, deadlift, press, clean) preserve performance best

      **Advanced programming models:**

      **1. Undulating periodization (off-season):**
      - Vary intensity session to session (heavy, light, moderate)
      - Prevents staleness, maintains interest
      - 4 lifts per week

      **2. Block periodization (in-season):**
      - Block 1: strength maintenance (weeks 1-6)
      - Block 2: power maintenance (weeks 7-12)
      - Block 3: peak/taper (playoffs)

      **3. Concurrent model (year-round):**
      - Lift 2-4x per week depending on phase
      - Sprint work 1-3x per week depending on phase
      - Throwing volume managed carefully

      **Testing benchmarks:**

      | Test | High School Average | College Average | Pro |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | 60-yard dash | 7.2-7.5 sec | 6.9-7.1 sec | 6.5-6.8 sec |
      | Exit velocity | 85-90 mph | 90-95 mph | 95+ mph |
      | Throwing velocity (outfield) | 75-85 mph | 85-90 mph | 90+ mph |
      | Vertical jump | 22-26 in | 26-30 in | 30+ in |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Train year-round but cycle emphasis by season
      - 2x per week in-season, low volume, high quality
      - 4-6x per week in off-season, progressive overload
      - Rotational power work 2x per week (off-season), 1x per week (in-season)
      - Sprint work 2-3x per week (off-season), 1x per week (in-season)
      - Arm care every session
      - Take 2-4 weeks off from throwing each year
      - Test and reassess every 6-8 weeks

# Baseball / Softball Training

Baseball and softball are rotational sports. Strength, power, speed, and arm health drive performance. Train all four, and protect the shoulder.
