  - level: 1
    title: "What Does Flag Football Demand Physically?"
    body: |
      Flag football looks like tackle football. It's actually a different sport.

      **No collisions. No linemen. No piles.** Instead, flag football is a game of space, speed, and skill. Every player has to run, cut, and pursue.

      **The physical demands:**

      - **Speed and acceleration** -- every position needs to get open or chase someone down
      - **Change of direction** -- cutting, juking, breaking on the ball
      - **Repeated sprint ability** -- plays are longer and more continuous than tackle
      - **Aerobic capacity** -- less stoppage time means more running
      - **Explosive power** -- jumping for contested catches, breaking away
      - **Lean body composition** -- speed and agility reward a high power-to-weight ratio

      **The big differences from tackle football:**

      - No neck training (no collisions)
      - No mass-building priority (heavy isn't better)
      - No lineman strength demands (there aren't linemen)
      - More emphasis on aerobic conditioning (fewer whistles, longer plays)
      - More emphasis on agility and cutting

      **What stays the same:**

      - Acceleration and max velocity
      - Explosive power
      - Change of direction
      - Hamstring and knee injury prevention
      - General strength as the foundation

      **The base:** squat, deadlift, single-leg work, sprints. But the emphasis is speed.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Train for Flag Football"
    body: |
      **The three pillars:**

      **1. Speed and acceleration**
      - 10-20m sprints 2-3x per week, full recovery
      - 30-40m sprints for top-end speed 1-2x per week
      - Resisted sprints (sled, band) for force development
      - Block starts for first-step quickness
      - This is the #1 priority

      **2. Change of direction and agility**
      - 5-10-5 shuttle, pro-agility, L-drill
      - Cone drills (cutting, weaving)
      - Sport-specific: route running, break-on-ball drills
      - 2-3 sessions per week

      **3. Strength and power base**
      - Back squat, deadlift, RDL
      - Single-leg work (split squats, single-leg RDL)
      - Power clean, trap bar jump, med ball throws
      - Pull-up, press, row
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **Sample off-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Lower body + speed |
      | Tue | Conditioning + agility |
      | Wed | Rest / mobility |
      | Thu | Speed + COD |
      | Fri | Upper body + power |
      | Sat | Skill work / open play |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **Sample in-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Full body (low volume) + speed |
      | Tue | Practice / game |
      | Wed | Recovery + mobility |
      | Thu | Full body (low volume) |
      | Fri | Game |
      | Sat | Game |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **In-season rule:** 2 lifts per week, 45 min max. Practice provides speed and conditioning work. Weight room is for maintenance and injury prevention.

      **Injury problem:** even without collisions, flag football has high rates of hamstring and ankle injuries from cutting and sprinting. Nordic curls and single-leg work are non-negotiable.

      **Body composition matters:** heavier isn't better in flag. Lean and fast wins.

  - level: 3
    title: "The Science of Flag Football Performance"
    body: |
      Flag football is a distinct sport, though research on it is thinner than tackle football or soccer. The physical profile sits between tackle football skill positions and soccer.

      **The energy system profile:**

      Unlike tackle football (which has 20-40 second stoppages between plays), flag football has more continuous movement:

      - Play length: 5-10 seconds (longer than tackle)
      - Rest between plays: 15-30 seconds
      - Continuous movement: 25-40% of game time
      - Sprint count: 30-50 per game

      This means flag football demands more aerobic fitness than tackle football, less than soccer. It's a mixed aerobic-anaerobic sport.

      **Physical determinants of performance:**

      - **Acceleration (10-20m):** the single most important quality
      - **Max velocity (30-40m):** for breakaways and deep routes
      - **Change of direction:** cutting, breaking on the ball, evading defenders
      - **Repeated sprint ability:** recover between plays and stay fast all game
      - **Vertical jump:** contested catches, defensive pass breakups
      - **Lean body composition:** power-to-weight ratio drives speed

      **What flag football does NOT need:**

      - Maximal 1RM strength (no blocking, no tackling)
      - Mass building (heavier = slower)
      - Neck training (no collisions)
      - Heavy eccentric work (no contact forces)

      **Programming differences vs. tackle football:**

      | Aspect | Tackle Football | Flag Football |
      |---|---|---|
      | Strength emphasis | High (especially linemen) | Moderate |
      | Speed emphasis | High | Very high |
      | Conditioning emphasis | Moderate | High |
      | Mass building | Priority for linemen | Avoid |
      | Neck training | Essential | Not needed |
      | COD emphasis | High | Very high |
      | Collision prep | Essential | None |

      **Injury profile:**

      Despite no collisions, flag football has notable injuries:

      - **Hamstring strains (~30%)** -- sprinting injuries, most common
      - **Ankle sprains (~25%)** -- cutting, landing
      - **Knee injuries (~15%)** -- cutting, landing (ACL, MCL)
      - **Groin strains (~10%)** -- cutting, sprinting

      **Evidence-based prevention:**

      - **Nordic curls:** 2-3x per week (reduces hamstring injury ~50%)
      - **Single-leg balance:** 3x per week (ankle prevention)
      - **Copenhagen planks:** 2-3x per week (groin prevention)
      - **Landing mechanics:** plyometric progression (ACL prevention)
      - **Hip strengthening:** side planks, band work

      **Position-specific demands (typical 5v5 or 7v7):**

      | Position | Physical Demands |
      |---|---|
      | Rusher | Acceleration, change of direction, agility |
      | Center | Snap accuracy, quickness, route running |
      | Receiver | Acceleration, top speed, cutting |
      | Running back | Acceleration, power, cutting |
      | Safety / DB | Backpedal, break on ball, COD |
      | Quarterback | Arm strength, mobility, decision-making |

      **Programming implications:**

      - Base lifts: back squat, deadlift, RDL, single-leg work
      - Power: power clean, trap bar jump, med ball throws
      - Speed: 3-4 sessions per week off-season -- this is the priority
      - COD: 2-3 sessions per week
      - Conditioning: 2-3 sessions per week (mixed aerobic-anaerobic)
      - Injury prevention: every session
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance
      - Lean body composition: prioritize power-to-weight

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Flag Football Performance Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, flag football performance sits between tackle football (skill positions) and soccer. It's a speed-endurance sport with high change-of-direction demands.

      **The physical profile:**

      Flag football has limited research, but the pattern is clear from adjacent sports:

      **From tackle football (skill positions):**
      - Acceleration and max velocity
      - Change of direction
      - Explosive power
      - Hamstring and knee injury prevention

      **From soccer:**
      - Aerobic base (more continuous play than tackle)
      - Repeated sprint ability
      - Ankle stability

      **The combination makes flag football a hybrid sport.** Training must develop both sprint speed and aerobic fitness, with high COD emphasis.

      **The physical demands in detail:**

      - **Sprint count:** 30-50 per game
      - **Sprint distance:** 10-40m (broader range than tackle)
      - **COD events:** 50-80 per game
      - **High-intensity distance:** 1-2 km per game
      - **Total distance:** 4-6 km per game

      **Force-velocity profile:**

      Flag football rewards a **velocity-dominant** profile:

      - High max velocity
      - Quick acceleration
      - Fast force production (RFD)
      - Moderate max strength

      The training focus is speed development, not max strength. Squat 1.5-2.0x BW is plenty. Beyond that, diminishing returns for flag football.

      **The conditioning question:**

      Flag football conditioning is often undertrained. Athletes assume it's like tackle (short bursts), but the continuous movement demands more aerobic fitness.

      Best conditioning for flag football:
      - **Repeated sprint intervals:** 6-10x 20-30 sec hard / 30-60 sec easy
      - **Small-sided games:** 3v3, 4v4 for sport-specific conditioning
      - **Tempo runs:** 20-30 min at moderate pace
      - **Interval training:** 4x4 min at 90-95% max HR

      **Advanced programming models:**

      **1. Speed-first model (off-season):**
      - 3-4 speed sessions per week
      - 2-3 strength sessions per week
      - 2 conditioning sessions per week
      - High total training volume, lean body composition focus

      **2. Undulating (in-season):**
      - Day 1: speed + strength (moderate)
      - Day 2: recovery / mobility
      - Day 3: power + speed
      - Day 4: game or practice
      - Weekly rotation maintains both qualities without overtraining

      **3. Concurrent (year-round):**
      - Speed work 2-3x per week
      - Strength 2x per week
      - Conditioning via small-sided games
      - Appropriate for athletes who play year-round

      **The body composition angle:**

      Flag football rewards a lean, powerful body. Unlike tackle football where mass moves mass, flag football punishes extra weight.

      Target physique:
      - Low body fat (8-15% for males, 15-22% for females)
      - High relative strength (squat 1.5-2.0x BW)
      - High vertical jump (28-36 inches)
      - Fast 10-20m sprint

      **Testing benchmarks:**

      | Test | High School | College | Elite |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | 10m sprint | 1.8-2.0 sec | 1.7-1.85 sec | 1.65-1.8 sec |
      | 30m sprint | 4.4-4.7 sec | 4.2-4.4 sec | 4.0-4.2 sec |
      | 40yd dash | 4.7-5.1 sec | 4.5-4.7 sec | 4.3-4.5 sec |
      | 5-10-5 shuttle | 4.6-5.0 sec | 4.4-4.6 sec | 4.2-4.4 sec |
      | Vertical jump | 22-28 in | 28-32 in | 32-38 in |
      | Broad jump | 7-8 ft | 8-9 ft | 9-10 ft |
      | Back squat | 1.3-1.6x BW | 1.6-1.9x BW | 1.8-2.1x BW |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Speed is the priority -- train it more than strength
      - Lean body composition -- don't chase mass
      - Conditioning is often undertrained -- don't skip it
      - COD work 2-3 sessions per week off-season
      - Nordic curls 2-3x per week -- non-negotiable
      - Single-leg work 2-3x per week
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance
      - Test every 6-8 weeks, adjust based on transfer
      - Sleep 8-9 hours

# Flag Football Training

Flag football is a speed sport. No collisions, no linemen, but high demands on acceleration, change of direction, and conditioning. Train speed first.
