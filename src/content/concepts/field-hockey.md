    title: "What Does Field Hockey Demand Physically?"
    body: |
      Field hockey looks like a stick sport. It's actually a running sport with a stick in your hand.

      **The physical demands of field hockey:**

      - **Endurance** -- a game is 60 minutes of near-continuous running with only brief stoppages
      - **Repeated sprint ability** -- constant transitions from jog to sprint
      - **Change of direction** -- cutting, dodging, reversing at speed
      - **Low body position** -- most of the game is played bent over
      - **Rotational power** -- hitting, pushing, and flicking the ball
      - **Sprint speed** -- breakaways and defensive recovery
      - **Core stability** -- holding a low, athletic position while moving

      **The engine matters most.** Field hockey is a 60-minute aerobic-dominated sport with repeated anaerobic bursts. Both systems are trained.

      **The specific demands are also unique:**
      - Playing bent over means heavy eccentric load on the lower back
      - Cutting and dodging mean hip and ankle stability are critical
      - Repeated rotational hits mean the obliques and hip rotators are heavily involved
      - The sport is asymmetric -- one side dominates

      **The base:** general strength in the weight room. Squat, deadlift, press, pull. Build the engine alongside it.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Train for Field Hockey"
    body: |
      **The four pillars:**

      **1. General strength**
      - Back squat, front squat, deadlift, RDL
      - Single-leg work (split squats, lunges) -- very important for cutting
      - Bench press, press, row, pull-up
      - Core anti-rotation (Pallof press) -- for hit and flick
      - Build in the off-season, maintain in-season

      **2. Conditioning**
      - Aerobic base: 30-60 min LISS 2-3x per week off-season
      - Repeated sprint ability: 6-10x 20-30 sec hard / 30-60 sec easy
      - Sport-specific: 60-sec shuttle runs, small-sided games
      - 3-4 sessions per week off-season

      **3. Speed and change of direction**
      - 10-20m sprints for acceleration
      - Cutting drills (5-10-5 shuttle, pro-agility)
      - Lateral movement drills (defensive slide)
      - 2-3 sessions per week off-season

      **4. Hip and ankle stability**
      - Single-leg work: Bulgarian split squats, single-leg RDL
      - Lateral band walks
      - Ankle stability (balance work, single-leg hops)
      - Nordic curls for hamstring health
      - Every session

      **Sample off-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Lower body + speed |
      | Tue | Conditioning + core |
      | Wed | Rest / mobility |
      | Thu | Lower body + power |
      | Fri | Conditioning + speed |
      | Sat | Sport-specific drills + skill work |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **Sample in-season week:**

      | Day | Session |
      |---|---|
      | Mon | Full body (low volume) |
      | Tue | Practice / game |
      | Wed | Recovery + mobility |
      | Thu | Full body (low volume) |
      | Fri | Game |
      | Sat | Game |
      | Sun | Rest |

      **In-season rule:** 2 lifts per week, 45 min max, no soreness-inducing work. Preserve the legs for the pitch.

      **The low body position problem:** playing bent over for 60 minutes is a real load. Train the lower back with anti-extension core work (planks, dead bugs) and hip hinge work (RDL, good mornings) to protect it.

  - level: 3
    title: "The Science of Field Hockey Performance"
    body: |
      Field hockey is a running sport first. The physical qualities that predict success are the same ones that predict success in soccer and other continuous-movement team sports -- with hockey-specific demands layered on top.

      **The energy system profile:**

      Research on field hockey matches (Spencer et al., 2004; Lythe & Kilding, 2011) shows:

      - Total distance covered per game: 5-7 km
      - High-intensity running: 15-25% of total distance
      - Sprint count: 40-60 per game
      - Average sprint distance: 10-20m
      - Work-to-rest ratio: ~1:2 to 1:4

      This means field hockey is **aerobic-dominant with a significant anaerobic component.** Training must develop both.

      **Physical determinants of performance:**

      - **VO2max** (aerobic capacity) -- correlates with total distance covered and work rate
      - **Repeated sprint ability** -- correlates with high-intensity running late in the game
      - **10-20m sprint speed** -- correlates with breakaway and recovery speed
      - **Change of direction ability** -- correlates with defensive and offensive effectiveness
      - **Lower body strength** -- correlates with sprint speed and COD ability
      - **Hip and ankle stability** -- correlates with injury prevention

      **Injury profile (female-dominant sport):**

      Field hockey has a high injury rate -- especially in females:
      - Ankle sprains (most common)
      - ACL injuries (elevated vs. males)
      - Hamstring strains
      - Low back pain (from bent-over position)

      **Prevention strategies (evidence-based):**

      - Nordic curls 2-3x per week (hamstring)
      - Single-leg balance and stability work (ankle)
      - Neuromuscular training programs (ACL -- reduce risk 50%+)
      - Hip mobility and core anti-extension work (low back)
      - Landing mechanics training (ACL)

      **Position-specific demands:**

      | Position | Primary Physical Demands |
      |---|---|
      | Forward | Acceleration, change of direction, sprint speed |
      | Midfield | Highest total distance, endurance, repeated sprints |
      | Defender | Change of direction, lateral quickness, strength |
      | Goalkeeper | Reaction, flexibility, lateral power |

      **Programming implications:**

      - Base lifts: back squat, deadlift, RDL, press, pull-up
      - Single-leg focus: split squats, single-leg RDL, step-ups
      - Conditioning: 3-4 sessions per week off-season, mostly aerobic with intervals
      - Speed: 2-3 sessions per week off-season
      - Injury prevention: every session (Nordics, balance, landing)
      - In-season: 2x per week maintenance
      - Deload every 6-8 weeks off-season, 4-6 weeks in-season

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Field Hockey Performance Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, field hockey performance is a running-economy problem with specific injury prevention priorities.

      **The running demand in detail:**

      GPS tracking studies (Gabbett, 2010; Lythe & Kilding, 2011) quantify field hockey demands precisely:

      - **Total distance:** 5-7 km per 60-min game
      - **High-intensity distance (>15 km/h):** 800-1,500m
      - **Sprint distance (>25 km/h):** 200-400m
      - **Sprint count:** 40-60 efforts
      - **Average sprint distance:** 10-20m
      - **Average sprint duration:** 2-4 sec
      - **Work-to-rest ratio:** ~1:2 to 1:4

      **Positional differences:**

      | Position | Total Distance | High-Intensity | Sprints |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Midfield | 6-7 km | Highest | 50-60 |
      | Defender | 5-6 km | Moderate | 40-50 |
      | Forward | 5-6 km | High | 45-55 |
      | Goalkeeper | 2-3 km | Low | Minimal |

      **Physical determinants of performance:**

      **Aerobic:**
      - VO2max of 55-60 ml/kg/min in elite female field hockey players
      - Correlates with total distance covered

      **Anaerobic:**
      - Repeated sprint ability (RSA) -- ability to maintain sprint speed across many efforts
      - Correlates with high-intensity running late in the game

      **Neuromuscular:**
      - 10-20m sprint speed
      - Change of direction ability
      - Reactive agility

      **Injury prevention -- the priority:**

      Field hockey has an injury rate of 6-10 per 1,000 hours of play. The most common:

      - **Ankle sprains (30%):** inversion injuries from cutting and landings
      - **ACL injuries (15%):** especially in females, from cutting and landing
      - **Hamstring strains (15%):** from sprinting
      - **Low back pain (10%):** from bent-over position

      **Evidence-based prevention (FIFA 11+, IOC programs):**

      - **Nordic curls:** 2-3x per week, reduces hamstring injury ~50%
      - **Single-leg balance:** 3x per week, reduces ankle sprains
      - **Landing mechanics:** teaches valgus prevention
      - **Neuromuscular training:** reduces ACL injuries 50-70% in females
      - **Hip mobility and core work:** reduces low back pain

      **Advanced programming models:**

      **1. Polarized training (off-season):**
      - 80% of conditioning at low intensity (Zone 2)
      - 20% at high intensity (intervals, sprints)
      - Avoids the moderate-intensity "dead zone"

      **2. Block periodization:**
      - Block 1: aerobic base (4 weeks)
      - Block 2: speed and RSA (4 weeks)
      - Block 3: sport-specific prep (2-3 weeks)
      - Block 4: taper and in-season (1-2 weeks)

      **3. Concurrent model (in-season):**
      - 2 lifts per week
      - 1-2 conditioning sessions (or use practice as conditioning)
      - 1-2 speed sessions (or use practice)
      - Focus on maintenance, not development

      **The "low body position" adaptation:**

      Playing bent over for 60 min per game creates a specific demand:
      - Sustained isometric contraction of the erector spinae and glutes
      - Eccentric loading during cutting in a flexed position
      - Adaptation requires training these positions

      Training to protect the low back:
      - Hip hinge work (RDL, good mornings) 1-2x per week
      - Anti-extension core work (planks, dead bugs) 2-3x per week
      - Hip flexor mobility work daily
      - Thoracic mobility work daily

      **Testing benchmarks:**

      | Test | High School | College | Elite |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | 10m sprint | 1.9-2.0 sec | 1.8-1.9 sec | 1.75-1.85 sec |
      | 30m sprint | 4.5-4.8 sec | 4.3-4.5 sec | 4.1-4.3 sec |
      | 5-10-5 shuttle | 5.0-5.4 sec | 4.7-5.0 sec | 4.4-4.7 sec |
      | Yo-Yo IR1 | 15-17 | 17-19 | 19+ |
      | Back squat 1RM | 1.0-1.3x BW | 1.3-1.6x BW | 1.6-2.0x BW |

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Train year-round; aerobic base in off-season, speed in pre-season
      - 3-4 conditioning sessions per week in off-season
      - 2-3 speed sessions per week in off-season
      - Every session: single-leg work, hip stability, core
      - Nordic curls 2-3x per week -- non-negotiable
      - Single-leg balance and landing work 3x per week
      - In-season: maintain with 2x per week
      - Test every 6-8 weeks, adjust based on transfer
      - Never train so hard you can't perform on the pitch

# Field Hockey Training

Field hockey is a running sport with a stick. Endurance, repeated sprint ability, and change of direction drive performance. Protect the ankles, hamstrings, and low back.
