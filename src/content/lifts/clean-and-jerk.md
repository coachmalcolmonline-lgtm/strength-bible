    title: "What Is the Clean & Jerk?"
    body: |
      The clean & jerk is two lifts in one. First, you clean the bar from the floor to your shoulders. Then, you jerk it from your shoulders to overhead.

      It's the second of the two Olympic lifts. The snatch is one motion; the clean & jerk is two.

      **The clean:** floor to shoulders. Same as a power clean, but you catch in a **full squat** -- below parallel, not above.

      **The jerk:** shoulders to overhead. You dip slightly, drive the bar up with your legs, then split your feet or push under it to catch overhead with locked elbows.

      **Why it matters:**

      - Highest total load lifted overhead in sport
      - Trains lower-body power AND upper-body stability
      - Combines strength, speed, mobility, and coordination
      - The classic test of an athlete's full-body power

      Most athletes train power clean or power snatch rather than clean & jerk, because the jerk adds a technique layer that requires dedicated coaching. But for athletes with the time and coaching, the clean & jerk is the most complete power-building lift.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform the Clean & Jerk"
    body: |
      **Part 1 -- The Clean**

      Same five positions as the power clean, but you catch in a full squat (below parallel).

      **Setup through the second pull:** identical to the power clean.

      **Turnover and catch (full clean):**

      - Aggressive pull under after the second pull
      - Fast elbows -- rotate them around the bar
      - Drop under the bar into a full front squat
      - Bar rests on the front delts, elbows high
      - Chest upright, back flat, weight mid-foot

      **Recovery:**

      - Stand up from the squat, keeping the bar racked on the shoulders

      **Part 2 -- The Jerk**

      Two main styles:

      **Split jerk (competition standard):**

      - Dip: unlock the knees, dip 4-6 inches straight down (chest upright)
      - Drive: extend hips and knees violently, launching the bar up
      - Split: one foot forward, one back, catching the bar overhead with locked elbows
      - Recovery: bring the front foot back, then the back foot forward

      **Push jerk (simpler, less load):**

      - Dip and drive like a split jerk
      - Re-bend the knees and catch the bar overhead in a partial squat
      - Stand to finish

      **Primary Points of Performance:**

      - Dip is straight down, not forward
      - Drive is vertical, chest stays upright
      - Split stance is even -- front shin vertical, back knee slightly bent
      - Bar overhead in line with ears
      - Elbows locked at the catch
      - Feet land simultaneously (split) or together (push)

      **Common Faults:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Dip drifts forward | Keep chest up, weight mid-foot |
      | Jerk press-out | Drive harder, get under faster |
      | Split uneven | Land front shin vertical, back knee soft |
      | Bar drifts behind | Bar over ears, not behind head |
      | Elbows soft | "Punch the ceiling" cue |

  - level: 3
    title: "The Biomechanics of the Clean & Jerk"
    body: |
      The clean & jerk is a two-stage power event. It's the heaviest overhead lift in sport -- elite lifters jerk 2.5x bodyweight overhead.

      **Clean biomechanics:**

      - Force production: peak vertical GRF of 2.5-3.5x bodyweight during the second pull
      - Bar path: identical to power clean, but continues to a full-squat catch
      - Catch depth: below parallel (hip crease below knee)
      - Front rack position: bar on front delts with elbows high

      **Jerk biomechanics:**

      - Dip depth: 4-8 cm (~15% of leg length) for optimal power transfer
      - Drive velocity: peak vertical bar velocity of 1.3-1.8 m/s
      - Split stance: front foot ~25-30 cm forward, back foot ~25-30 cm back
      - Front shin: vertical at catch (if the shin is angled forward, the athlete is trying to press)
      - Overhead lockout: elbows fully extended, wrists neutral, bar over ears

      **The dip-drive sequence (jerk):**

      1. Eccentric loading phase: dip down (~0.3 sec)
      2. Isometric/amortization: brief transition at the bottom
      3. Concentric drive: explosive extension (~0.2 sec)
      4. Bar flight: the drive is over before the split
      5. Split catch: feet land, bar settles overhead

      Total time from dip start to split catch: ~0.6-0.8 seconds.

      **Force-velocity considerations:**

      - The clean produces ~30-40 W/kg peak power
      - The jerk produces ~25-35 W/kg
      - Combined, the clean & jerk is the second-highest power-producing lift in the sport (behind the snatch)

      **Programming implications:**

      - Clean and jerk can be trained in separate sessions (clean one day, jerk another)
      - Jerk recovery is faster than clean recovery -- some programs jerk more often
      - Never jerk with tired legs -- the dip-drive requires fresh CNS
      - 1-3 reps per set, 5-8 sets, 2-3 min rest
      - Video every rep -- bar path and split stance both matter

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Clean & Jerk Science"
    body: |
      At the research level, the clean & jerk is studied as the ultimate test of full-body power and technical precision under maximal load.

      **Neuromuscular demands:**

      The clean & jerk demands near-maximal motor unit recruitment across two distinct explosive efforts, separated by a brief recovery (the stand-up from the front squat). This is unique among barbell movements.

      Key neural factors:
      - Rate coding in the second pull: 60+ Hz
      - Rate coding in the jerk drive: 55-65 Hz
      - Intermuscular coordination across >25 muscles
      - Recovery within a single rep: the lift is essentially two max-effort events with ~1-2 seconds between

      **Hormonal response:**

      Heavy clean & jerk sessions produce:
      - Elevated testosterone
      - Elevated growth hormone
      - Cortisol increase (catabolic stress marker)
      - These responses are similar to other heavy compound lifts, but the C&J produces the largest acute GH spike of any resistance exercise

      **Energy system demand:**

      The clean & jerk is dominated by the ATP-PC (phosphagen) system -- each rep is 3-5 seconds of max effort. Rest between sets must be 2-3 min for full PCr restoration. Aerobic contribution is minimal.

      **Technical variations:**

      **Split jerk vs. power jerk vs. squat jerk:**

      - **Split jerk:** standard; allows the most load; requires balance and split-stance mobility
      - **Power jerk (push jerk):** feet stay together; simpler; less load
      - **Squat jerk:** catch overhead in a full squat; requires elite mobility; used by a minority of lifters

      **Clean variations:**

      - **Full clean:** catch below parallel (standard competition)
      - **Power clean:** catch above parallel
      - **Hang clean:** start from knee or thigh
      - **Block clean:** start from blocks at knee or thigh height

      **Programming models for advanced athletes:**

      **1. Competition prep:**
      - 6-8 weeks out: high volume, moderate intensity (70-80%)
      - 3-4 weeks out: moderate volume, high intensity (85-95%)
      - 1-2 weeks out: low volume, near-max intensity, then taper
      - Test day: 1RM attempts

      **2. Concurrent with strength:**
      - 2 clean & jerk sessions/week
      - 1 heavy (85%+), 1 light (70-80%)
      - Combine with back squat, front squat, and pull work

      **3. Technique-focused blocks:**
      - 4-6 weeks of sub-maximal training (60-75%)
      - Emphasis on positions and bar path
      - Video analysis every session

      **Testing and benchmarking:**

      | Level | C&J 1RM (as % BW) |
      |---|---|
      | Beginner | 0.5× BW |
      | Intermediate | 0.9× BW |
      | Advanced | 1.3× BW |
      | Elite | 1.7× BW+ |

      A 180 lb athlete clean & jerking 162 lbs is intermediate. Advanced is 234 lbs. Elite is 306+ lbs.

      **Program design considerations:**

      - Clean and jerk recovery demand is high; space 48 hours apart
      - Jerk training can be separated -- some programs jerk 3-4x per week with light cleans
      - Front squat is the single most transferable accessory lift
      - Overhead pressing (strict press, push press) builds the jerk's stability base
      - Snatch balance and overhead squat build jerk confidence

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Master the split jerk before chasing max loads
      - Front squat 1.5x bodyweight before pushing heavy cleans
      - Overhead press 0.75x bodyweight before pushing heavy jerks
      - Video every rep -- bar path and split stance are both unforgiving
      - Never jerk when tired -- the drive is a CNS demand, not a muscular one
      - Peak at the right time -- 2-week taper before a test or competition

# Clean & Jerk

The clean & jerk is the heaviest overhead lift in sport -- floor to shoulders, then shoulders to overhead. Two explosive efforts in one lift. The gold standard for total-body power.
