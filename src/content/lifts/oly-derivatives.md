  - level: 1
    title: "What Are Olympic Lift Derivatives?"
    body: |
      The snatch and the clean & jerk are the two Olympic lifts. But most athletes don't need to compete in them. They need the **power, speed, and coordination** those lifts build.

      Derivatives are partial versions of the Olympic lifts. They keep the explosive parts and drop the technical difficulty. Most athletes who "do Olympic lifts" are actually doing derivatives.

      **The main derivatives:**

      - **Power clean** -- floor to shoulders, caught above parallel
      - **Power snatch** -- floor to overhead in one motion, caught above parallel
      - **Hang clean / hang snatch** -- start from knee or thigh height instead of the floor
      - **High pull** -- like a clean or snatch, but you don't catch it -- just pull the bar high
      - **Jump shrug** -- minimal arm bend, maximal hip extension
      - **Clean pull / snatch pull** -- the pull without the catch

      **Why they matter:**

      - Every derivative trains hip and knee extension -- the same pattern as jumping and sprinting
      - Lower technique demand means you can train them more often
      - Loads can be higher without risking technical breakdown
      - Transfer to sport is equal or better for most athletes

      **The rule:** train the derivative that matches your sport's demand. Sprinters and jumpers do hang power cleans and jump shrugs. Throwers do clean pulls. Team-sport athletes do power cleans and power snatches.

  - level: 2
    title: "The Main Derivatives and How to Use Them"
    body: |
      **1. Power Clean**

      - Setup: floor, standard clean grip
      - Pull: same as full clean
      - Catch: above parallel (thighs above horizontal)
      - Use for: general power development, most athletes

      **2. Power Snatch**

      - Setup: floor, wide snatch grip
      - Pull: same as full snatch
      - Catch: above parallel, bar overhead
      - Use for: athletes who need overhead power and can handle the mobility demand

      **3. Hang Clean / Hang Snatch**

      - Setup: bar at knee or thigh, standing
      - Pull: short, explosive -- no first pull from floor
      - Catch: same as power clean / power snatch
      - Use for: reducing lower-back stress, focusing on second pull technique, in-season work

      **4. High Pull (Clean or Snatch)**

      - Setup: same as clean or snatch
      - Pull: full extension, aggressive shrug, elbows high
      - Catch: none -- bar doesn't come overhead or to shoulders
      - Use for: reducing technical load, training the explosive pull without the catch

      **5. Jump Shrug**

      - Setup: bar at hip level, clean grip
      - Movement: dip slightly, extend hips explosively, shrug shoulders, come onto toes
      - No elbow bend
      - Use for: teaching hip extension, building explosive power, warm-up for full lifts

      **6. Clean Pull / Snatch Pull**

      - Setup: same as clean or snatch
      - Pull: full extension, shrug, and pull the bar up -- but don't catch it
      - Load: 90-110% of clean / snatch 1RM
      - Use for: building pull strength, teaching bar path, overloading the movement

      **How to program them:**

      | Goal | Derivative | Sets x Reps | Load |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | General power | Power clean | 5x3 | 70-80% |
      | Explosive power | Hang power clean | 4x3 | 60-75% |
      | Max pull strength | Clean pull | 4x3 | 90-105% |
      | Teach hip extension | Jump shrug | 3x5 | 30-50% |
      | In-season maintenance | Hang high pull | 3x3 | 60-70% |
      | Snatch-focused | Power snatch | 5x2-3 | 65-80% |

      **Warm-up progression (Burgener Warm-up pattern):**

      Before any Olympic lift, do:
      - Down and up (dip and drive)
      - Elbows high and outside (clean grip)
      - Muscle clean
      - Muscle snatch
      - Tall clean
      - Tall snatch

      These build positions and timing without load.

  - level: 3
    title: "Biomechanics of Derivatives"
    body: |
      Derivatives train the same neuromuscular pathways as full Olympic lifts with modified technique demands. Here's how each works biomechanically.

      **Power clean vs. full clean:**

      - Same pull, different catch
      - Power clean catch: above parallel (thighs above horizontal)
      - Full clean catch: below parallel
      - Power clean loads: 70-85% of full clean 1RM
      - Neuromuscular demand: nearly identical for the pull phase

      **Hang vs. floor:**

      - Hang versions eliminate the first pull (floor to knee)
      - The athlete starts at the "power position" (bar at mid-thigh)
      - Emphasis shifts to the second pull and turnover
      - Lower-back load is reduced by ~30-40%
      - Bar velocity is similar if load is adjusted

      **High pull vs. clean:**

      - High pull drops the catch -- the entire lift is the pull
      - Peak bar velocity is similar or higher than clean
      - Loads can be 10-15% higher than clean 1RM
      - Trains the shrug + extension without the technical catch

      **Jump shrug:**

      - Isolation of the hip extension + shrug
      - Minimal arm bend -- bar stays close
      - Loads: 30-50% of clean 1RM
      - Used as a warm-up and teaching tool

      **Clean / snatch pulls:**

      - Overloaded pull (90-110% 1RM)
      - Trains the pull with sub-maximal velocity -- the load is heavier than a max clean
      - Develops strength-endurance for the pull pattern
      - Requires excellent technique -- bad positions under heavy load are dangerous

      **The "power position" and its role:**

      The power position -- hips flexed, knees slightly bent, torso upright, bar at mid-thigh -- is the key position for all derivatives. Everything from there is explosive extension. Athletes who can hit this position consistently can hit all derivatives.

      **Force-velocity implications:**

      | Derivative | Peak Power | Peak Force | Velocity |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Jump shrug | Moderate | Moderate | High |
      | Power clean | High | High | High |
      | Hang power clean | High | Moderate | High |
      | Clean pull | Moderate | Very high | Low-moderate |
      | High pull | High | Moderate | Very high |

      Programming implications: pair derivatives by intent. Use jump shrugs and high pulls for velocity. Use clean pulls for force. Use power cleans for the blend.

      **Testing benchmarks:**

      | Lift | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Power clean | 0.5x BW | 0.8x BW | 1.2x BW |
      | Power snatch | 0.4x BW | 0.6x BW | 0.9x BW |
      | Hang power clean | 0.4x BW | 0.7x BW | 1.0x BW |
      | Clean pull | 0.7x BW | 1.0x BW | 1.4x BW |

      Test every 6-8 weeks under consistent conditions.

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Derivative Programming"
    body: |
      At the research level, derivatives are studied for their specific transfer to athletic performance and their dose-response characteristics.

      **Transfer studies:**

      - **Power clean to sprint acceleration:** significant transfer, effect size ~0.5-0.7 in trained athletes. Greater transfer to 0-10m acceleration than max velocity.
      - **Power clean to vertical jump:** effect size ~0.4-0.6. Strength base moderates transfer.
      - **Hang power clean to change of direction:** effect size ~0.3-0.5. Relevant for team sport athletes.
      - **Clean pull to 1RM clean:** effect size ~0.7. Used as a strength builder for the pull phase.

      **Dose-response:**

      Research (Suchomel et al., 2019; Comfort et al., 2018) supports:
      - 3-5 sets per session is optimal for power derivatives
      - 2-4 reps per set (below fatigue threshold)
      - 70-85% 1RM for power clean, 60-75% for hang versions
      - 2-3 min rest between sets for full PCr restoration
      - 2-3 sessions per week during power emphasis
      - 1-2 sessions per week during maintenance

      **Periodization models:**

      **1. Traditional periodization:**
      - Off-season: 3 sessions/week, moderate volume
      - Pre-season: 2 sessions/week, moderate intensity
      - In-season: 1 session/week, low volume, high quality
      - Peak/competition: drop or minimal maintenance

      **2. Undulating (DUP):**
      - Vary intensity session to session
      - Day 1: heavy power cleans (85%)
      - Day 2: light hang cleans (65%)
      - Day 3: moderate power snatches (75%)
      - Maintains adaptation without staleness

      **3. Block periodization:**
      - Block 1 (4 weeks): accumulate volume, moderate intensity
      - Block 2 (3 weeks): intensify, reduce volume
      - Block 3 (2 weeks): peak, low volume, high intensity
      - Test at end of block 3

      **The "power position" mastery test:**

      An elite athlete can hit the power position from any derivative -- jump shrug, hang clean, power clean -- with the same bar path and timing. This is the standard for technical mastery.

      **Common advanced programming mistakes:**

      - **Too much volume.** Derivatives are CNS-demanding. More than 6-8 sets per session is overkill.
      - **Too heavy.** Above 85% 1RM, the pull slows and the movement stops being explosive.
      - **Too little recovery.** 48 hours between heavy derivative sessions.
      - **Too much variation.** Pick 2-3 derivatives and master them.
      - **Skipping the pull.** Forgetting clean pulls and snatch pulls means the strength base for the pull never develops.
      - **Not enough technique work.** Even at advanced levels, warm-up with Burgener warm-up and light technique reps.

      **Testing and benchmarking:**

      Elite athletes are typically tested on:
      - Power clean 1RM or 3RM
      - Power snatch 1RM or 3RM
      - Vertical jump (transfer proxy)
      - 10m sprint (transfer proxy)
      - Broad jump (transfer proxy)

      Track all five. If power clean is going up but jumps aren't, the transfer is broken -- likely due to insufficient strength base or insufficient velocity.

      **Programming takeaways for advanced athletes:**

      - Match derivative to goal: hang for in-season, floor for off-season, pulls for strength, high pulls for velocity.
      - Train derivatives on fresh legs, 2-3 sessions per week.
      - Overload the pull (90-105%) once per week.
      - Test every 6-8 weeks; adjust based on transfer.
      - Combine with sprint and jump work for maximum transfer.
      - Video every rep -- positions are the entire point.
