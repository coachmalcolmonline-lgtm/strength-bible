    title: "What Is the Bridge?"
    body: |
      The bridge is a bodyweight exercise where you lie on your back, plant your feet, and lift your hips toward the sky.

      Simple? Yes. Powerful? Also yes.

      **Why it matters:**

      - Builds the posterior chain (glutes, hamstrings, lower back)
      - Trains hip extension -- the movement behind every jump, sprint, and lift
      - Opens up the hips and thoracic spine
      - Requires zero equipment
      - Scales from beginner to advanced

      **The posterior chain problem:** most athletes are quad-dominant. They squat and lunge, but their glutes and hamstrings are weak. The bridge fixes that.

      Bridges are not just a warm-up. Done heavy (single-leg, weighted) they build serious hip strength.

      **Scaling note:** If the standard bridge is easy, progress to single-leg. If single-leg is too hard, stick with two legs and add a pause at the top. The scaled version is not less -- it's the rung you're on.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform the Bridge"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Lie on your back
      - Feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart, close to your butt
      - Arms at your sides (or extended overhead for a shoulder bridge)

      **Execution:**
      1. Squeeze your glutes and press through your heels
      2. Lift your hips toward the sky
      3. Stop when your body forms a straight line from knees to shoulders
      4. Hold for 1-2 seconds at the top
      5. Lower under control
      6. Repeat

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Hips drive up, not forward
      - Glutes do the work (not the lower back)
      - Knees track over feet (no caving in)
      - Full lockout at the top (straight line from knee to shoulder)
      - Controlled descent

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Lower back arching to compensate | Cue "Ribs down, squeeze glutes harder" |
      | Hips driving forward not up | Cue "Push through heels, hips straight up" |
      | Knees caving in | Cue "Knees out over toes" |
      | Not reaching full lockout | Cue "Full hip extension -- squeeze hard at top" |

      **Progressions:**
      - Glute bridge (two legs) -> single-leg glute bridge -> feet elevated single-leg -> weighted single-leg
      - Shoulder bridge: hands overhead, push into floor
      - Hip thrust (barbell): loaded version of the same movement
      - Marching bridge: alternate lifting each foot while hips stay up

      **Reps:** 3x10-15 for conditioning, 3x5 per side for strength (single-leg).

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Glute and hamstring activation (warm-up, pre-lift)
      - Posterior chain strength (single-leg variations)
      - Mobility work (opens hips, thoracic spine)
      - Lower back rehab (when done correctly)

      **Programming:**
      - Warm-up activation: 2x10 glute bridges before lower body sessions
      - Strength work: 3-4x5-8 per side single-leg bridges
      - Mobility work: 2-3x30 sec holds with thoracic extension
      - Hypertrophy: 3x12-15 barbell hip thrusts

      **What it trains:**
      - Gluteus maximus (primary hip extensor)
      - Hamstrings (assist hip extension)
      - Erector spinae (isometric stabilization)
      - Adductors (stabilize the hips)
      - Core (anti-extension as the hips rise)

      **Why it matters for athletes:**
      - Hip extension powers every sprint, jump, and lift
      - Weak glutes lead to hamstring strain injuries
      - Glute activation improves squat and deadlift performance
      - Bridges are a low-risk way to build posterior chain volume

      **Benchmarks:**
      - 30 unbroken glute bridges: baseline
      - 10 single-leg bridges per side: intermediate
      - Weighted hip thrust 1.5x BW: advanced
      - Weighted hip thrust 2x BW: strong

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Muscle activation:**
      - Gluteus maximus: primary driver of hip extension
      - Gluteus medius: stabilizes the hip (single-leg variations)
      - Hamstrings: assist hip extension, co-contract with glutes
      - Erector spinae: isometric stabilization of the spine
      - Rectus abdominis and obliques: anti-extension as hips rise

      **EMG research:**
      - Barbell hip thrust (loaded bridge) produces the highest gluteus maximus activation of any common exercise -- higher than squats or deadlifts.
      - Single-leg glute bridges produce higher glute activation than bilateral versions due to increased stability demand.
      - Adding a pause at the top of the bridge increases time under tension and hypertrophic stimulus.

      **Variations for specific adaptations:**
      - **Glute bridge (basic):** baseline, warm-up, activation
      - **Single-leg glute bridge:** unilateral strength, hip stability
      - **Feet-elevated bridge:** increased range of motion and hamstring involvement
      - **Shoulder bridge (hands overhead):** adds thoracic extension and shoulder mobility
      - **Marching bridge:** anti-rotation core work
      - **Barbell hip thrust:** loaded version, gold standard for glute hypertrophy
      - **Frog pump:** short-range high-volume glute isolation

      **Programming science:**
      - Glutes respond to both heavy loads (5-8 reps) and higher reps (12-20).
      - Hip thrusts are a staple in powerlifting and physique programming for glute development.
      - Bridges are also used in rehab (low back pain, hip pain, glute amnesia).
      - For athletes: hip thrust 1.5-2x BW for sprinting and jumping carryover.
      - Combine with RDLs, Nordic curls, and squats for full posterior chain development.
