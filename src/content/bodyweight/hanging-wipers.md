    title: "What Are Hanging Wipers?"
    body: |
      Hanging wipers are an advanced core exercise done from a pull-up bar. You hang, raise your legs to horizontal, then rotate them side to side like windshield wipers.

      It looks simple. It isn't.

      **Why it matters:**

      - Trains the obliques and rotational core strength
      - Requires significant grip endurance
      - Demands shoulder stability and control
      - Builds toward advanced gymnastics movements
      - Directly transfers to any sport with rotation (throwing, swinging, cutting)

      **This is not a beginner exercise.** To do hanging wipers, you need:

      - A 30+ second dead hang
      - The ability to hold your legs at horizontal for several seconds
      - Strong obliques
      - Healthy shoulders and elbows

      If you can't do those yet, start with **floor wipers** -- the same rotation pattern, lying on your back, no hang required. Floor wipers are not less than hanging wipers. They're the rung below. Same ladder.

      **Scaling note:** The floor wiper -> plank wiper -> hanging knee tuck -> bent-knee wiper -> straight-leg wiper is the ladder. Pick your rung. Master it. Climb.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform Hanging Wipers"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Hang from a pull-up bar, hands shoulder-width apart, overhand grip
      - Active hang (shoulders engaged, not just hanging limp)
      - Legs straight and together

      **Execution (bent-knee wiper -- the scaled version):**
      1. From the hang, raise your knees to chest height
      2. Keeping the knees bent, rotate your hips and knees to one side
      3. Control the rotation -- don't swing
      4. Return to center
      5. Rotate to the other side
      6. Return to center -- that's one rep

      **Execution (straight-leg wiper -- the full version):**
      1. From the hang, raise straight legs to horizontal (L-position)
      2. Rotate the legs side to side like windshield wipers
      3. Control both directions, no momentum
      4. Keep the legs straight the whole time
      5. Each side-to-side is one rep

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Full hang to start
      - Core braced throughout
      - Rotation comes from the obliques, not from swinging
      - Legs stay under control at all times
      - Reset between reps

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Swinging instead of rotating | Slow down, control the descent |
      | Only rotating one direction well | Practice the weaker side more |
      | Legs dropping on rotation | Reduce range of motion until stronger |
      | Grip failing early | Build dead hangs and grip endurance |
      | Losing shoulder engagement | Cue "Shoulders down and back" |

      **Progressions:**
      - Floor wipers (lying on back)
      - Plank wipers (forearm plank)
      - Dead hang hold (30-60 sec)
      - Hanging knee tucks (10+ reps)
      - Bent-knee wipers (this level)
      - Straight-leg wipers (full version)
      - Weighted wipers

      **Reps:** 3x5-8 per side (both directions). Quality over quantity.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Rotational core strength
      - Oblique development
      - Grip endurance under dynamic load
      - Shoulder stability under asymmetric load
      - Progresses toward advanced gymnastics movements

      **Programming:**
      - Volume: 3x5-8 per side
      - Frequency: 2-3x per week max
      - Warm-up: 2x5 hanging knee tucks before wipers
      - Pair with pull-ups or hanging knee tucks in the same session
      - Deload every 4-6 weeks (grip and obliques need recovery)

      **What it trains:**
      - External and internal obliques (rotation)
      - Rectus abdominis (pelvic stability)
      - Hip flexors (legs at horizontal)
      - Latissimus dorsi (active hang)
      - Forearm flexors (grip)
      - Shoulder stabilizers (control during rotation)

      **Why it matters for athletes:**
      - Rotational core strength transfers to throwing, swinging, cutting
      - Anti-rotation strength transfers to resisting tackles
      - Grip endurance carries over to carries, deadlifts, pull-ups
      - Shoulder stability under load is critical for throwing athletes

      **Benchmarks:**
      - 3x5 bent-knee wipers per side: intermediate
      - 3x5 straight-leg wipers per side: strong
      - 3x8 straight-leg wipers with 5 sec hold per side: advanced
      - Weighted straight-leg wipers: elite

      **Scaling for beginners:**
      - If you can't hang: dead hang practice (5x20-30 sec)
      - If you can't hold your legs at horizontal: work on hanging knee tucks first
      - If bent-knee wipers are too hard: reduce range of motion
      - If straight-leg wipers are too hard: keep knees bent

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Muscle activation:**
      - External and internal obliques: primary rotators
      - Rectus abdominis: anti-extension during leg lift
      - Hip flexors: hold the legs at horizontal
      - Latissimus dorsi: active hang position
      - Serratus anterior: scapular control
      - Forearm flexors: grip

      **EMG research:**
      - Rotational core exercises produce the highest oblique activation of any abdominal exercise.
      - Hanging rotational work adds anti-gravity and grip demand on top of the rotation, making it significantly more demanding than floor-based variations.
      - The straight-leg version requires near-maximal hip flexor activation to maintain the horizontal position while rotating.

      **Why the scaling ladder matters here:**

      Hanging wipers are one of the highest-skill, highest-strength core movements in gymnastics. The ladder from floor wipers to straight-leg hanging wipers typically takes 6-18 months of consistent training to climb. Attempting to skip rungs is a fast track to shoulder, elbow, or lower back injury.

      The scaled versions are not "baby exercises." They are the same movement pattern, trained at progressively higher demand. Master each rung before progressing.

      **Variations for specific adaptations:**
      - **Floor wipers (lying on back):** entry-level, teaches rotation pattern
      - **Plank wipers:** adds core bracing under load
      - **Hanging bent-knee wipers:** introduces the hang + rotation
      - **Straight-leg hanging wipers:** full version
      - **Weighted wipers:** add ankle weights for progressive overload
      - **Single-arm wipers:** advanced -- adds anti-rotation demand on the hanging arm
      - **Windshield wiper to bar:** combines wipers with a toes-to-bar

      **Programming science:**
      - Rotational core work 2-3x per week is sufficient.
      - Warm up the shoulders thoroughly before any hanging rotation work.
      - Watch for asymmetric development -- do equal reps both directions.
      - Deload every 4-6 weeks; obliques and grip recover slower than larger muscle groups.
      - Pair with anti-rotation work (Pallof press, suitcase carries) for balanced core development.

      **Injury considerations:**
      - Lower back: if the lumbar spine rounds or the hips drop during rotation, stop the set.
      - Shoulders: any impingement or pinching means dropping to a lower rung.
      - Elbows: high-volume hanging work is a common cause of medial elbow pain.
      - Grip failure: always have a mat underneath the bar.

      **Testing:**
      - Measure max reps per side with strict form.
      - MDC (minimum detectable change) for hanging wiper reps is roughly 1 rep.
      - Test every 4-6 weeks under identical conditions.
