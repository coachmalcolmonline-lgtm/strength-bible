    title: "What Is Downward Facing Dog?"
    body: |
      Downward facing dog is a classic yoga position that found its way into athletic training for good reason. You start in a push-up position, then push your hips up and back until your body forms an inverted V.

      It looks simple. It stretches almost the entire posterior chain at once.

      **Why it matters:**

      - Stretches hamstrings, calves, and lower back simultaneously
      - Opens the shoulders and thoracic spine
      - Decompresses the spine (hips high, head down)
      - Warms up the shoulders for pressing or overhead work
      - Zero equipment

      **The pattern:** hips high, heels reaching toward the floor, hands pressing into the ground, chest reaching toward the thighs. Everything stretches at once.

      **The reality:** most athletes can't get their heels to the floor on day one. That's fine. The position works at whatever range you have.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform Downward Facing Dog"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Start in a push-up position (hands on floor, arms straight)
      - Hands shoulder-width apart, feet hip-width apart

      **Execution:**
      1. Push your hips up and back
      2. Body forms an inverted V
      3. Press your hands into the floor
      4. Reach your heels toward the floor (they don't have to touch)
      5. Let your head hang naturally
      6. Hold 20-60 seconds
      7. Lower back to a push-up position or come to your knees

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Hips high (highest point of the body)
      - Arms straight, hands pressing into the floor
      - Chest reaching toward the thighs
      - Heels reaching toward the floor
      - Head relaxed, neck long
      - Breathe slowly into the stretch

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Hips too low | Cue "Push the hips higher" |
      | Back rounding | Cue "Long spine, chest to thighs" |
      | Elbows bending | Cue "Straight arms, press the floor away" |
      | Trying too hard to touch heels | Cue "Bend the knees, focus on the spine" |
      | Holding breath | Cue "Slow breath, hold the position" |

      **Progressions:**
      - Downward dog with bent knees
      - Downward dog with straight legs
      - Downward dog with heels touching floor
      - Downward dog with feet elevated
      - One-leg downward dog

      **Reps:** 2-3 holds of 20-60 seconds.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Posterior chain mobility
      - Warm-up before pressing or overhead work
      - Cool-down after posterior chain training
      - Spinal decompression

      **Programming:**
      - 2-3 holds of 20-60 seconds
      - Can be done before or after training
      - Daily if desired
      - Pair with cat-cow and child's pose for a mini-flow

      **What it trains:**
      - Hamstrings
      - Calves
      - Lower back
      - Shoulders
      - Thoracic spine (extension)
      - Glutes

      **Why it matters for athletes:**
      - Counterbalances sitting posture
      - Prepares the shoulders for overhead work
      - Relieves posterior chain tightness
      - Decompresses the spine after heavy loading

      **Benchmarks:**
      - Downward dog with bent knees, 30 seconds: baseline
      - Downward dog with straight legs, 45 seconds: good
      - Downward dog with heels touching floor, 60 seconds: strong
      - One-leg downward dog, 30 seconds each: advanced

      **Scaling:**
      - If hands/wrists hurt: use blocks or fists
      - If hamstrings are tight: bend the knees
      - If too intense: use a wall (hands on wall, hips back)
      - If pregnant: widen stance and avoid full inversion

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Mobility demands addressed:**
      - Hamstrings: stretched as hips push back
      - Calves: stretched as heels reach toward floor
      - Lower back: decompressed and stretched
      - Shoulders: loaded in flexion
      - Thoracic spine: extended as chest reaches forward
      - Glutes: stretched as hips flex

      **Why it's a universal mobility drill:**
      - Loads and stretches the entire posterior chain plus shoulders at once
      - Accessible to almost every athlete (with scaling)
      - Doubles as a shoulder warm-up and hamstring stretch

      **Programming notes:**
      - Great as first or last mobility drill of the session
      - Progress by lowering heels or lifting one leg
      - Regress by bending knees, using blocks, or performing against a wall
      - Breathe slowly -- stretch deepens on the exhale

      **Research context:**
      - Downward dog is a mild inversion, which can improve parasympathetic tone
      - It stretches hamstrings in a position more functional than a seated forward fold
      - Combining it with cat-cow produces more improvement than either alone

      **Variations:**
      - **Bent-knee:** entry-level
      - **Straight-leg:** full posterior chain stretch
      - **Wall:** hands on wall, hips back
      - **One-leg:** unilateral hamstring emphasis
      - **On blocks:** easier on wrists and shoulders
      - **Three-legged dog flow:** dynamic transition

      **When NOT to do it:**
      - Acute wrist injury: use fists or blocks
      - Acute shoulder injury: consult a professional
      - High blood pressure: consult doctor
      - Pregnancy: widen stance

      **Pairs well with:**
      - Cat-cow
      - Child's pose
      - Upward facing dog
      - Plow
