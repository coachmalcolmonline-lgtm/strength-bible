  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform It"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Start standing

      **Execution:**
      1. Step forward with your right foot into a lunge
      2. Place your left hand on the ground inside your right foot
      3. Rotate your right arm up toward the sky, opening your chest
      4. Hold for 2–3 seconds
      5. Return hand to ground
      6. Step back to standing
      7. Repeat on the other side

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Deep lunge (front knee over ankle, back leg extended)
      - Rotation through the thoracic spine (not the lumbar)
      - Core tight
      - Controlled movement
      - Full range of motion

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Front knee collapsing in | Cue "Knee out over toes" |
      | Rotation coming from lower back | Cue "Rotate from the upper back" |
      | Back leg sagging | Cue "Back leg straight, heel lifted" |
      | Rushing through it | Cue "Slow down, hold the rotation" |

      **Reps:** 5–8 reps per side in a warm-up.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Pre-workout mobility: opens hips, ankles, thoracic spine.
      - Pre-lift prep: gets the athlete into positions they'll need under load.
      - Post-workout cool-down: reduces stiffness, promotes recovery.

      **Programming:**
      - 5–8 reps per side in warm-up.
      - 3–5 reps per side in cool-down.
      - Can be done daily.

      **What it trains:**
      - Hip flexor mobility (lunge)
      - Hamstring mobility (straight back leg)
      - Thoracic rotation
      - Ankle dorsiflexion
      - Core stability (during rotation)

      **Why it's called the World's Greatest:**
      - Hits 5+ major mobility areas in one drill
      - Requires no equipment
      - Scales from beginner to advanced
      - Fast enough for daily warm-up
      - Effective enough to produce real change over time

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Mobility demands addressed:**
      - **Hip flexors:** stretch on the back leg with hip extended.
      - **Hamstrings:** stretch on the front leg as you lunge forward.
      - **Thoracic spine:** rotation of the upper body.
      - **Ankle dorsiflexion:** front ankle flexed forward.
      - **Glenohumeral rotation:** open arm rotating upward.

      **Why compound mobility drills matter:**
      - Isolated stretches address one area at a time.
      - Compound drills like the World's Greatest Stretch train mobility in coordination with stability — the way the body actually moves.
      - Produces faster warm-up, better movement quality, and lower injury risk.

      **Programming notes:**
      - Best done first in a warm-up sequence.
      - Follow with a more specific drill (pass-throughs, ankle work, hip activation).
      - Progress by deepening the lunge or increasing the rotation.
      - Regress by reducing the range of motion or using a wall for balance.

      **Research context:**
      - Dynamic mobility work outperforms static stretching before training for maintaining power output while improving range of motion.
      - Compound mobility drills are more time-efficient than isolated stretches.
      - The World's Greatest Stretch is a "bang for your buck" mobility drill used in many athletic warm-up protocols.

      **Variations:**
      - **World's Greatest Stretch #2:** Adds a hamstring stretch and different arm position.
      - **With dowel:** Use a dowel for shoulder rotation.
      - **With rotation hold:** Hold the thoracic rotation for 3–5 seconds for a deeper stretch.
