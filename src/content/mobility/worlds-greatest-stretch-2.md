      The World's Greatest Stretch #2 is the sibling of #1. It hits the same major mobility areas -- hips, hamstrings, thoracic spine, ankles -- but adds a hamstring focus and a different arm reach.

      **Why you need both:** #1 opens the front side of the hips. #2 opens the back side. Athletes who only do #1 end up with a still-tight posterior chain. Doing both gives balanced hip mobility.

      It's the second half of the "world's greatest" warm-up duo.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform It"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Start standing

      **Execution:**
      1. Step forward with your right foot into a lunge
      2. Place both hands on the ground inside your right foot
      3. Keep your left (back) leg straight -- feel the hamstring stretch
      4. Reach your right arm forward and up, opening the chest and thoracic spine
      5. Hold for 2-3 seconds
      6. Return hand to ground
      7. Step back to standing
      8. Repeat on the other side

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Back leg stays straight (hamstring stretch)
      - Deep lunge on the front leg
      - Rotation through the thoracic spine (not the lower back)
      - Core tight
      - Full range of motion

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Back leg bending | Cue "Straight back leg, heel lifted" |
      | Rotation coming from lower back | Cue "Rotate from the upper back" |
      | Front knee collapsing in | Cue "Knee out over toes" |
      | Rushing through it | Cue "Slow down, hold the rotation" |

      **Reps:** 5-8 reps per side in a warm-up.

      **Difference from WGS #1 at a glance:**

      | Feature | WGS #1 | WGS #2 |
      |---|---|---|
      | Front hip opening | Deep lunge | Deep lunge |
      | Back leg emphasis | Hip flexor stretch | Hamstring stretch |
      | Arm reach | Rotate up and open | Reach forward and up |
      | Best for | Hip flexors, thoracic | Hamstrings, thoracic |

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Pre-workout mobility: opens hips, hamstrings, thoracic spine
      - Pre-lift prep: gets the athlete into positions they'll need under load
      - Post-workout cool-down: reduces stiffness, promotes recovery

      **Programming:**
      - 5-8 reps per side in warm-up
      - 3-5 reps per side in cool-down
      - Can be done daily
      - Pair with WGS #1 for complete hip + hamstring prep

      **What it trains:**
      - Hamstring mobility (back leg straight)
      - Hip flexor mobility (front leg deep lunge)
      - Thoracic rotation (reach)
      - Ankle dorsiflexion (front ankle flexed forward)
      - Core stability (during rotation)

      **Why pair it with #1:**

      WGS #1 and #2 are designed as a pair:
      - #1 opens the front of the hip (hip flexors)
      - #2 opens the back of the hip (hamstrings)
      - Both add thoracic rotation
      - Together, they hit 90% of the mobility issues that block athletes

      Two drills, five minutes, full-body prep. That's the value.

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Mobility demands addressed:**
      - Hamstrings: stretch on the back leg as you lunge forward (the key difference from #1)
      - Hip flexors: stretch on the front leg
      - Thoracic spine: rotation of the upper body
      - Ankle dorsiflexion: front ankle flexed forward
      - Glenohumeral rotation: open arm reaching forward and up
      - Core: stability during rotation

      **Why compound mobility drills matter:**
      - Isolated stretches address one area at a time.
      - Compound drills like WGS #2 train mobility in coordination with stability -- the way the body actually moves.
      - Produces faster warm-up, better movement quality, and lower injury risk.
      - Combining #1 and #2 is more efficient than isolated hip and hamstring stretches.

      **Programming notes:**
      - Best done in the middle of the warm-up sequence after a light aerobic primer.
      - Follow with a more specific drill (pass-throughs, ankle work, hip activation).
      - Progress by deepening the lunge or increasing the rotation.
      - Regress by reducing the range of motion or using a wall for balance.

      **Research context:**
      - Dynamic mobility work outperforms static stretching before training for maintaining power output while improving range of motion.
      - Hamstring mobility in particular is important for athletes: tight hamstrings increase strain injury risk and reduce hip hinge depth.
      - Combining hip and hamstring mobility drills produces more durable range of motion than either alone.

      **Variations:**
      - **With dowel:** Use a dowel for the arm reach and shoulder rotation.
      - **With rotation hold:** Hold the thoracic rotation for 3-5 seconds for a deeper stretch.
      - **Elevated back foot:** Increases the hamstring stretch on the back leg.
      - **Single-arm version:** Removes the ground contact for a balance challenge.

      **When NOT to do it:**
      - Acute hamstring injury: do not stretch an injured hamstring. Wait for clearance.
      - Fresh post-surgery: consult with a medical professional.
      - During competition warm-up: this is a prep drill, not a performance drill -- keep the range moderate.
