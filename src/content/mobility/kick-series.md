    title: "What Is the Kick Series?"
    body: |
      The kick series is a dynamic hip and leg mobility drill. You stand tall, hold onto something for balance, and swing each leg through a series of controlled kicks -- front, side, and back.

      It looks simple. It's one of the most effective ways to open the hips before training.

      **Why it matters:**

      - Opens hip flexors, hamstrings, adductors, and glutes
      - Warms up the hip joint through full range of motion
      - Improves coordination between the leg and the trunk
      - Prepares the hips for squats, deadlifts, sprints, and kicks
      - Zero equipment

      **The pattern:** front kicks (hip flexion), side kicks (abduction), back kicks (hip extension). Three directions, all through the hip. Together they cover the full mobility spectrum of the hip joint.

      **The important thing:** control, not speed. Fast, sloppy kicks do nothing. Slow, controlled kicks open range and teach the nervous system new positions.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform the Kick Series"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Stand tall, feet together
      - Hold onto a wall, rack, or pole for balance
      - Keep the standing leg slightly bent
      - Engage your core

      **Execution (each direction, 10 reps per side):**

      **Front kicks:**
      1. Swing one leg forward and up (knee straight or slightly bent)
      2. Kick to a height you can control
      3. Lower with control
      4. Repeat 10 times

      **Side kicks:**
      1. Swing the same leg out to the side
      2. Keep the torso upright
      3. Control the descent
      4. Repeat 10 times

      **Back kicks:**
      1. Swing the leg backward
      2. Extend the hip without arching the lower back
      3. Control the return
      4. Repeat 10 times

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Upright torso
      - Core braced
      - Controlled swing (no momentum-driven kicks)
      - Full range but not to the point of pain
      - Equal reps both sides

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Swinging too fast | Slow down, control the descent |
      | Leaning back | Keep torso upright, engage core |
      | Only going one direction | Do all three: front, side, back |
      | Skipping the weaker side | Equal reps both sides |
      | Hips rotating with the kick | Stabilize the pelvis, keep it square |

      **Reps:** 10 each direction per side = 30 total kicks per leg.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Hip mobility opener
      - Movement prep before lower-body work
      - Warm-up before sprinting, kicking, or jumping
      - Cool-down after heavy leg training

      **Programming:**
      - 10 reps each direction per side (front, side, back)
      - 2-3 minutes total per leg
      - Before lower body training, sprinting, or sport practice
      - Can be done daily

      **What it trains:**
      - Hip flexors (front kicks)
      - Hip abductors (side kicks)
      - Hip extensors and adductors (back kicks)
      - Adductors (side kicks)
      - Standing-leg stability (balance)
      - Core stability (keeping the torso upright)

      **Why it matters for athletes:**
      - Hip range of motion is a limiting factor in squats, deadlifts, and sprints
      - Kicking sports (soccer, martial arts, football) demand full hip ROM
      - Tight hips increase injury risk in the lower back and knees
      - Dynamic hip work before training improves movement quality
      - Standing on one leg while kicking is a real balance and stability challenge

      **Benchmarks:**
      - Front kick to hip height with straight leg: baseline
      - Front kick to chest height with straight leg: good
      - Side kick to hip height with straight leg: baseline
      - Side kick to shoulder height: good
      - Back kick with full hip extension and no lumbar arch: good

      **Scaling:**
      - If hip pain: reduce range to a pain-free zone
      - If balance is the issue: hold onto a wall with both hands
      - If mobility is limited: perform the kicks lying down (supine leg swings)

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Mobility demands addressed:**
      - Hip flexion: front kicks open the front of the hip capsule
      - Hip abduction: side kicks open the lateral hip
      - Hip extension: back kicks stretch the hip flexors and open the front hip capsule
      - Adductor mobility: side kicks stretch the inner thigh
      - Standing-leg stability: single-leg balance under dynamic load

      **Why dynamic kicks beat static hip stretches:**
      - Static stretches before training reduce power output (known since the 1990s).
      - Dynamic mobility drills improve range of motion WITHOUT reducing power.
      - Kicks combine range of motion with coordination and balance -- three adaptations in one drill.
      - The pattern is closer to real athletic movement than a static stretch.

      **Programming notes:**
      - Perform after a light aerobic primer (jog, bike) and before strength work.
      - Pair with World's Greatest Stretch and pass-throughs for a complete warm-up.
      - Progress by increasing kick height or reducing hand support.
      - Regress by lying down and performing leg swings.

      **Research context:**
      - Dynamic warm-up protocols that include hip mobility work improve sprint speed, jump height, and change of direction in trained athletes.
      - Hip mobility is one of the strongest predictors of squat depth and posterior chain function.
      - Athletes with restricted hip mobility have higher rates of lower back and hip injuries.

      **Variations:**
      - **Standing with support:** baseline (wall or rack)
      - **Standing unsupported:** advanced balance challenge
      - **Supine leg swings:** floor-based, easier on balance
      - **With ankle weights:** loaded mobility for advanced athletes
      - **Slow eccentric:** emphasize the controlled return

      **When NOT to do it:**
      - Acute hip or groin injury: rest and see a professional.
      - Post-surgery: medical clearance required.
      - Fresh hamstring strain: front kicks stretch the hamstring -- wait until cleared.
      - Immediately before max-effort sprints: warm up first, don't go straight into max kicks.

      **Pairs well with:**
      - World's Greatest Stretch #1 and #2 (combined hip + hamstring)
      - Pass-throughs (shoulder prep)
      - Cossack squat (hip opening under load)
