    title: "What Is the Mobility Bridge?"
    body: |
      The mobility bridge is the same position as the bodyweight bridge -- lie on your back, plant your feet, lift your hips -- but with a different intent.

      **Bodyweight bridge:** builds posterior chain strength (glutes, hamstrings, lower back).

      **Mobility bridge:** opens the front of the body -- hip flexors, quads, thoracic spine, and chest. The goal is range, not load.

      Same position. Different purpose. Both matter.

      **Why mobility bridges matter:**

      - Opens the front of the hips after hours of sitting
      - Stretches the hip flexors, quads, and abdominals
      - Extends the thoracic spine (upper back)
      - Prepares the body for overhead positions
      - Zero equipment

      **The signal you're looking for:** the front of your hips should feel a stretch. If your lower back is doing the work instead, the hips aren't opening -- you're just arching.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform the Mobility Bridge"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Lie on your back
      - Feet flat on the floor, hip-width apart, close to your butt
      - Arms at your sides, or extend them overhead for a deeper thoracic stretch

      **Execution:**
      1. Press through your heels and lift your hips toward the sky
      2. Do NOT squeeze your glutes hard -- this is mobility, not strength
      3. Focus on the front of your body opening: hip flexors, quads, abs, chest
      4. Hold for 20-60 seconds
      5. Breathe slowly and deeply into the front of your body
      6. Lower under control
      7. Repeat 2-3 times

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Hips drive up, not forward
      - Ribs stay down (don't let the ribcage pop up)
      - Breathe into the front of the body
      - Hold the position; don't pulse
      - Feel the stretch across the front, not just in the lower back

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Lower back arching | Cue "Ribs down, tilt pelvis" |
      | Glutes clenching | Cue "Relax the glutes, breathe into the hips" |
      | No stretch felt | Bring feet closer to butt; extend arms overhead |
      | Holding breath | Cue "Slow breath into the stretch" |

      **Progressions:**
      - Bridge with feet close to butt (baseline)
      - Bridge with arms extended overhead (deeper thoracic)
      - Bridge with one leg extended (unilateral hip flexor stretch)
      - Bridge with elevated feet (deeper hip flexor stretch)
      - Supported bridge with a block under the sacrum (passive hold)

      **Reps:** 2-3 holds of 20-60 seconds.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Opens the front of the body after sitting
      - Prepares for overhead work (press, jerk, snatch)
      - Post-workout recovery for the hip flexors and quads
      - Anti-sitting posture drill

      **Programming:**
      - 2-3 holds of 20-60 seconds
      - Before or after training
      - Daily if you sit a lot
      - Pair with a seated or kneeling hip flexor stretch

      **What it trains:**
      - Hip flexors (psoas, rectus femoris)
      - Quadriceps
      - Abdominals
      - Thoracic spine (extension)
      - Chest and shoulders (with arms overhead)
      - Ankle dorsiflexion

      **Why it matters for athletes:**
      - Tight hip flexors limit hip extension in squats and deadlifts
      - Tight thoracic spine blocks overhead lockout
      - Sitting for hours shortens the front of the body
      - Bridges reverse the damage of sitting

      **Benchmarks:**
      - Full bridge hold for 30 seconds with no back pain: baseline
      - Full bridge hold with arms overhead for 45 seconds: good
      - Full bridge with feet elevated, 60 seconds, chest open: strong

      **Scaling:**
      - If the lower back hurts: bring the feet closer and tilt the pelvis first
      - If nothing stretches: elevate the feet or extend arms overhead
      - If it's too intense: use a block under the sacrum for a passive hold

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Mobility demands addressed:**
      - Hip flexors: stretch as the hips lift and extend
      - Quadriceps (rectus femoris): stretches with hip extension
      - Abdominals: stretch with hip and thoracic extension
      - Thoracic spine: extends as the ribs lift
      - Chest and shoulders: extended with arms overhead
      - Ankles: dorsiflexion to keep feet flat

      **Why this position is uniquely valuable:**
      - Most mobility drills target one area. The bridge opens the entire anterior chain at once.
      - It's the antidote to sitting -- the opposite of the desk posture.
      - It's passive enough to hold for extended periods, active enough to require engagement.

      **The hip flexor vs. lower back problem:**

      When the lower back arches during the bridge instead of the hip flexors opening, the athlete is compensating. This is common in athletes with tight hip flexors.

      The fix:
      1. Tuck the pelvis first before lifting
      2. Keep the ribs down
      3. Bring the feet closer to the butt
      4. Reduce the height of the bridge

      **Research context:**
      - Sustained hip flexor stretch reduces hip flexor stiffness and improves hip extension range.
      - Thoracic extension mobility correlates with overhead reach and shoulder health.
      - Passive bridge holds (with a bolster) reduce sympathetic tone and can improve recovery.

      **Variations:**
      - **Basic bridge hold:** 30-60 seconds
      - **Bridge with arms overhead:** deeper thoracic extension
      - **One-leg extended bridge:** unilateral hip flexor stretch
      - **Feet elevated bridge:** deeper stretch
      - **Supported bridge with block:** passive, restorative

      **When NOT to do it:**
      - Acute lower back pain: rest and see a professional.
      - Lumbar disc injury: avoid extension positions.
      - SI joint dysfunction: consult with a professional.

      **Pairs well with:**
      - World's Greatest Stretch #1 and #2
      - Couch stretch (more aggressive hip flexor work)
      - Thoracic extension over a foam roller
      - Downward facing dog

# Bridge (Mobility)

The bodyweight bridge position, but held for range, not strength. Opens the front of the body -- hip flexors, quads, thoracic spine.
