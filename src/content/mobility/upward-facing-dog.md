    title: "What Is Upward Facing Dog?"
    body: |
      Upward facing dog is the mirror image of downward facing dog. Instead of hips high, chest reaching toward thighs, you're face down with chest lifted and hips low.

      It's a spinal extension position -- the opposite of a forward fold.

      **Why it matters:**

      - Opens the chest and front of the shoulders
      - Extends the thoracic spine (upper back)
      - Stretches the abdominals and hip flexors
      - Counterbalances the forward-hunched sitting posture
      - Pairs with downward dog for a full spinal mobility flow

      **The pattern:** lie face down, press hands into the floor, lift the chest while keeping the legs on the ground. The spine extends backward.

      **The reality:** most athletes have tight thoracic spines and poor extension mobility. Upward dog exposes that tightness -- which is why it's valuable.

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform Upward Facing Dog"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Lie face down on the floor
      - Hands under the shoulders, elbows close to the body
      - Legs together or hip-width apart
      - Tops of the feet on the floor

      **Execution:**
      1. Press through the hands and lift your chest off the floor
      2. Keep the legs pressed into the ground (thighs stay down)
      3. Straighten the arms as much as comfortable
      4. Draw the shoulders down and back
      5. Lift the chest up and forward
      6. Hold 15-30 seconds
      7. Lower with control

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Legs stay on the floor (key distinction from a push-up)
      - Shoulders drawn down and back (not shrugged up)
      - Chest lifts forward and up
      - Head remains neutral
      - Breathe into the chest

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Legs lifting off floor | Cue "Press the thighs down" |
      | Shoulders shrugging up | Cue "Shoulders down and back" |
      | Neck cranking back | Cue "Neutral neck, look forward" |
      | Lower back doing all the work | Cue "Lift from the chest, not the low back" |
      | Elbows flaring | Cue "Elbows close to the body" |

      **Progressions:**
      - Sphinx pose (forearms on floor, chest lifted) -- entry
      - Baby cobra (small lift, elbows slightly bent)
      - Upward dog (arms straighter, bigger lift)
      - Upward dog with one leg extended

      **Reps:** 2-3 holds of 15-30 seconds.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Thoracic spine extension mobility
      - Chest and shoulder opener
      - Counterbalance to forward-hunched posture
      - Warm-up before overhead work or pressing
      - Part of a spinal mobility flow (cat-cow, downward dog, upward dog)

      **Programming:**
      - 2-3 holds of 15-30 seconds
      - Can be done before or after training
      - Pair with downward dog for a full extension-flexion flow
      - 2-3x per week

      **What it trains:**
      - Thoracic spine (extension)
      - Chest (stretch)
      - Front of shoulders (stretch)
      - Abdominals (stretch)
      - Hip flexors (mild stretch)
      - Wrists (load-bearing)

      **Why it matters for athletes:**
      - Opens the chest for full overhead lockout
      - Counterbalances the forward-hunched sitting posture
      - Improves thoracic extension, critical for overhead pressing
      - Prepares the shoulders for press, jerk, snatch

      **Benchmarks:**
      - Sphinx pose hold 30 seconds: baseline
      - Baby cobra hold 20 seconds: good
      - Upward dog with straight arms 30 seconds: strong
      - Upward dog with one leg extended 20 seconds: advanced

      **Scaling:**
      - If wrists hurt: forearms down (sphinx pose)
      - If lower back hurts: reduce range, focus on chest lift
      - If too intense: sphinx pose is the perfect regression

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Mobility demands addressed:**
      - Thoracic spine: extension
      - Chest (pectorals): stretch
      - Front of shoulders (deltoids): stretch
      - Abdominals: stretch with spinal extension
      - Hip flexors: mild stretch
      - Wrists: load-bearing in extension

      **Why thoracic extension matters:**
      - Thoracic spine stiffness is a primary limiter for overhead position
      - Tight thoracic spine forces compensation from the lumbar spine (arching back) or shoulders
      - Improved thoracic extension means better overhead position, safer pressing, and better breathing mechanics

      **Programming notes:**
      - Progress by increasing the lift or extending the arms
      - Regress by lowering to forearms (sphinx pose)
      - Breathe deeply into the chest -- each exhale deepens the position
      - Pair with downward dog for a complete spinal flow

      **Research context:**
      - Thoracic spine extension mobility correlates with overhead reach and shoulder health
      - Chest opening reduces forward shoulder posture, which improves overhead pressing
      - Combining extension and flexion mobility drills (upward dog + downward dog) produces balanced spinal mobility

      **Variations:**
      - **Sphinx pose:** forearms down, chest lifted -- easiest
      - **Baby cobra:** small lift with bent arms
      - **Upward dog:** full position, arms straight
      - **Upward dog one-leg:** adds unilateral hip flexor stretch
      - **Upward dog with block:** easier on wrists

      **When NOT to do it:**
      - Acute lower back pain: reduce range or skip
      - Disc injury (extension intolerance): avoid
      - Acute shoulder injury: consult a professional
      - Wrist injury: use forearms (sphinx)

      **Pairs well with:**
      - Downward facing dog (opposite pattern)
      - Cat-cow (spinal mobility warm-up)
      - Child's pose (gentle forward fold)
      - Bridge mobility (related extension)
