    title: "What Are Pass-Throughs?"
    body: |
      Pass-throughs are a shoulder mobility drill. You hold a stick (or a broom handle, or a dowel) with a wide grip, arms straight, and rotate it from front to back over your head and back again.

      It sounds easy. It's not — the first time.

      Pass-throughs reveal shoulder tightness immediately. If your arms have to bend to get the stick past your head, you have work to do.

      **Why it matters:**
      - Develops shoulder mobility for pressing, snatching, jerking
      - Essential prep for the overhead squat
      - Reveals tight pecs, lats, and thoracic spine
      - Improves posture

  - level: 2
    title: "How to Perform Pass-Throughs"
    body: |
      **Setup:**
      - Stand tall, feet shoulder-width apart
      - Hold a dowel, PVC pipe, or broom handle with a wide grip
      - Arms straight — no bending

      **Execution:**
      - Hold the dowel in front of you, arms extended at shoulder height
      - Rotate the dowel up and over your head, keeping arms locked
      - Continue rotating the dowel down and behind you
      - Return to the front along the same path
      - Repeat

      **Primary Points of Performance:**
      - Arms locked (no bending)
      - Full range of motion (front to back and back to front)
      - Controlled movement
      - Core tight

      **Common Faults & Fixes:**

      | Fault | Fix |
      |---|---|
      | Arms bending | Cue "Lock arms" |
      | Grip too narrow | Widen grip |
      | Unable to complete the rotation | Work on shoulder mobility; start with wider grip |
      | Pain or pinching | Stop. See a professional if persistent. |

      **Grip progression:**
      Start wide enough that the pass-through is easy. As mobility improves, narrow the grip. The goal is a grip just wider than shoulder-width with locked arms. That's the snatch grip.

  - level: 3
    title: "Programming & Function"
    body: |
      **Training role:**
      - Warm-up for any overhead work (press, push press, jerk, overhead squat, snatch).
      - Shoulder mobility development.
      - Diagnostic tool — reveals restrictions in real time.

      **Programming:**
      - 10–15 reps in a warm-up, front to back and back to front.
      - Combine with other shoulder mobility drills (dislocates, wall slides, thoracic extension work).
      - Progress by narrowing the grip as mobility improves.
      - Use before every overhead training session.

      **What it trains:**
      - Thoracic extension
      - Shoulder flexion/extension (from full front to full back overhead arc)
      - Scapular upward rotation
      - Pectoral, lat, and thoracic flexibility

      **Benchmark:**
      - Arms locked, snatch-width grip, smooth front-to-back passes = excellent shoulder mobility
      - Wider grip, some effort = average
      - Bending arms = restriction

  - level: 4
    title: "Advanced Considerations"
    body: |
      **Anatomical demands:**
      - Pass-throughs require coordinated thoracic extension, scapular upward rotation, glenohumeral mobility, and pectoral/lat flexibility.
      - Any restriction in one of these areas will force compensation elsewhere (usually arm bending or lumbar extension).

      **Common restrictions:**
      - **Tight lats:** prevent full overhead reach.
      - **Tight pecs:** limit shoulder extension behind the body.
      - **Thoracic kyphosis:** reduces the arc available for the stick to pass over the head.
      - **Shoulder capsule tightness:** limits glenohumeral range.

      **Programming notes:**
      - Progressively narrow the grip only when the current width is comfortable.
      - Never force a narrower grip — this drives compensation.
      - Combine with specific mobility work for the limiting area (pec SMR, lat SMR, thoracic foam rolling).
      - The pass-through is a test AND a treatment. Regular practice produces actual mobility improvement, not just temporary range.

      **Relation to Olympic lifting:**
      - Snatch grip width in the overhead position requires the same mobility as a narrow pass-through.
      - If a pass-through is hard at snatch grip, the snatch itself will be limited by shoulder mobility.
      - Many athletes improve their snatch simply by improving pass-through ability.
