# Power Program

An 8-week power block built around **contrast training** — pairing heavy strength sets with explosive movements to exploit post-activation potentiation. Designed for athletes who need to be explosive, not just strong.

## Overview

Four sessions per week: two heavy strength days and two contrast/plyometric days. The block develops power across the entire force-velocity curve — from heavy grinding strength to max-velocity jumps.

**Block structure:**

- Weeks 1–2: Introduction — learn the movements, establish baselines
- Weeks 3–4: Build — higher plyometric volume, contrast pairs
- Week 5: Deload
- Weeks 6–7: Peak — max intensity, French contrast
- Week 8: Taper + testing

## Who It Is For

- Football, basketball, baseball, volleyball, wrestling, track athletes
- Anyone whose sport rewards jumping, throwing, or sprinting
- Athletes who've built a strength base
- Anyone in an off-season or general prep phase

**Not for:**
- Beginners (build strength first — you can't express what you don't have)
- Athletes with active knee, ankle, or hip injuries
- Anyone mid-season with high game volume

## Weekly Structure

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Contrast A — Lower body |
| Tuesday | Strength A — Upper body |
| Thursday | Contrast B — Lower body (different pair) |
| Friday | Strength B — Full body, power emphasis |

Wednesday, Saturday, Sunday are rest days.

## Contrast Training — What It Is

A contrast pair is a heavy lift followed by an explosive movement targeting similar muscles.

**Classic examples:**

- **Back Squat** (3 reps @ 85%) → rest 3 min → **Vertical Jump** (3 reps)
- **Trap Bar Deadlift** (3 reps @ 85%) → rest 3 min → **Broad Jump** (3 reps)
- **Bench Press** (3 reps @ 85%) → rest 3 min → **Med Ball Chest Throw** (3 reps)
- **Front Squat** (3 reps @ 80%) → rest 3 min → **Box Jump** (3 reps)

The heavy set primes the nervous system. The explosive set teaches it to fire fast. Together they build power better than either alone.

## Sample Session — Contrast A (Lower)

**Warm-up (15 min):**
- Dynamic mobility (World's Greatest Stretch, leg swings, hip openers)
- A-skips, B-skips, high knees, butt kicks
- Build-up to first heavy pair

**Contrast Pair 1:**
- **Back Squat** — 4×3 @ 85% 1RM
- Rest 3 minutes
- **Vertical Jump** — 4×3 (reset, full effort, step down)

**Contrast Pair 2:**
- **Trap Bar Deadlift** — 3×3 @ 85% 1RM
- Rest 3 minutes
- **Broad Jump** — 3×3

**Accessory:**
- **Split Squat** — 3×8 per leg @ RPE 7
- **Nordic Curl** — 3×5–8
- **Calf Raise** — 4×10–12

**Cooldown:** Walk, light mobility.

## Sample Session — Strength A (Upper)

1. **Bench Press** — 4×3–5 @ RPE 7–8
2. **Overhead Press** — 3×5 @ RPE 7
3. **Barbell Row** — 4×5–6 @ RPE 7
4. **Pull-up (weighted)** — 3×5–6 @ RPE 8
5. **Dumbbell Row** — 3×8–10
6. **Triceps + Bicep Superset** — 3×10–12

## Plyometric Progression

Build slowly. Plyometric volume is measured in ground contacts (each landing = 1 contact; depth jumps count as 2–3).

| Week | Contacts/Session | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40–60 | Low (box jumps, broad jumps) |
| 2 | 50–70 | Low-moderate |
| 3 | 60–80 | Moderate (add bounds) |
| 4 | 60–80 | Moderate |
| 5 (Deload) | 20–30 | Low |
| 6 | 80–100 | Moderate-high (depth jumps) |
| 7 | 80–100 | High (add French contrast) |
| 8 (Taper) | 20–30 | Low (test only) |

## Progression Rules

**Strength:**

- Weeks 1–4: Add 5–10 lbs per week on main lifts, RPE 7–8
- Weeks 6–7: Keep weights constant, focus on speed of execution
- Never go above RPE 8 on heavy sets. This is a power block, not a strength peak.

**Contrast pairs:**

- Weeks 1–2: 3 sets per pair, moderate weight
- Weeks 3–4: 4 sets per pair
- Weeks 6–7: 5 sets per pair, or upgrade to French contrast

**Plyometrics:** See table above.

## Force-Velocity Balance

This block trains the whole curve:

- **Heavy (85%+):** 4×3 back squat, trap bar deadlift
- **Moderate (60–80%):** 3×5 front squat, jump squat with load
- **Light (30–60%):** Med ball throws, loaded jumps
- **Bodyweight:** Max-effort jumps, sprints

You need all four. If you only train heavy, you'll be slow. If you only train jumps, you'll be weak. Both together = powerful.

## Testing Battery

Test at week 1 and week 8:

- Vertical jump
- Broad jump
- Med ball throw distance (chest pass, rotational)
- Power clean 3RM or 1RM (if you know the lift)
- 10m sprint

Meaningful improvements: 2cm vertical, 5cm broad, 1 lb med ball throw.

## Recovery

Power is the most CNS-demanding quality. Recovery matters more than volume.

- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours. This is not negotiable.
- **Protein:** 1.6–2.2 g/kg per day
- **Calories:** at or slightly above maintenance
- **48 hours minimum** between hard power sessions
- **Never train power on fatigued legs.** If you're sore from yesterday, skip the contrast pair and do strength work instead.

## Deload Protocol

**Week 5:**

- Cut volume 40–50%
- Keep intensity (same weights on heavy sets)
- Cut plyometric contacts to 30
- No contrast pairs — do straight strength work

**Week 8:**

- Taper — low volume, low intensity
- Test at end of week

## Common Pitfalls

- **Training power when tired.** The #1 mistake. Power requires a fresh CNS.
- **Too much plyometric volume.** More is not better. 100 contacts is plenty.
- **Skipping the strength work.** You can't express force you don't have.
- **Going heavy every week.** This is a power block, not a strength peak.
- **Landing hard.** Every jump must land soft and quiet. Loud landings = joint stress.

## When to Switch

- **After 8 weeks:** retest. Either repeat the block (with new 1RMs) or transition to a sport-specific or maintenance phase.
- **In-season:** replace one contrast day with sport practice.
- **If power stalls:** check force-velocity balance. Most athletes need more strength work, not more jumping.
