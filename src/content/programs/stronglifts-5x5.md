# StrongLifts 5x5

StrongLifts 5x5 is a linear progression program built on five barbell lifts,
three days per week, alternating two workouts. It is the simplest possible
barbell strength system — one barbell, five lifts, three days a week.

## Overview

The program alternates Workout A and Workout B on non-consecutive days.
Every session, the athlete adds weight to each lift. Squat volume is higher
than most programs (every session, 5x5), which accelerates leg development.

## Who It Is For

StrongLifts is for athletes who want a minimalist, proven strength program
with minimal complexity. It requires only five lifts and one piece of
equipment — a barbell. It is one of the highest-yield training approaches
for early-stage barbell development.

## Prerequisites

1. Complete all foundational concepts.
2. Master Back Squat, Bench Press, Deadlift, Press, and Barbell Row.
3. Establish starting weights that allow completion of all prescribed sets
   and reps with proper form at RPE 7.

## The Two Workouts

**Workout A**
- Back Squat 5x5
- Bench Press 5x5
- Barbell Row 5x5

**Workout B**
- Back Squat 5x5
- Press 5x5
- Deadlift 1x5

## Weekly Structure

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Workout A |
| Tuesday | Recovery / Mobility |
| Wednesday | Workout B |
| Thursday | Recovery / Mobility |
| Friday | Workout A |
| Saturday | Optional / Off |
| Sunday | Full Rest |

Next week: A, B, A → B, A, B. Alternating throughout.

## Progression Rules

**Add weight every session** you complete all sets and reps with proper form.

- Back Squat: +5 lbs per session
- Bench Press: +5 lbs per session
- Press: +5 lbs per session
- Barbell Row: +5 lbs per session
- Deadlift: +10 lbs per session

**When you stall (fail to complete prescribed reps):**

1. First stall: repeat the same weight next session.
2. Second stall at same weight: deload 10% and work back up.
3. Third stall: switch programs or specialize.

## Autoregulation in Practice

StrongLifts is deliberately rigid, but autoregulation still applies:

- On a bad day, take the smallest increase or repeat a session.
- Form is non-negotiable. If form breaks, do not add weight.
- RPE should stay around 7-8 on working sets.

## Sample 12-Week Progression

Week 1-4: Linear progression, +5/+10 each session.
Week 5-8: Continued progression, first stalls likely.
Week 9-10: Resets as needed.
Week 11-12: Resets or program transition.

## Starting Weights

Common recommendation: start light — 50% of what you think you can lift —
and add weight every session. This gives 8-12 weeks of progression before
stalling.

## When to Switch Programs

Move on when:

1. You have stalled multiple times and resets no longer work.
2. You want more variety in programming.
3. You want to specialize.

Options: Texas Method, Wendler 5/3/1, PPL, or Smolov.

## Common Pitfalls

- Starting too heavy (kills progression in week 2).
- Skipping warm-up sets.
- Adding weight when form breaks down.
- Not eating enough.
- Switching programs prematurely.

## Related Programs

- Starting Strength — similar structure, less squat volume.
- Texas Method — the natural successor.
- Wendler 5/3/1 — monthly cycle progression for intermediates.
