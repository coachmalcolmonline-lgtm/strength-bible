# Weight Management Program

An 8-week program for gaining muscle, losing fat, or maintaining weight while improving body composition. Combines a strength-focused training block with nutrition targets and tracking.

## Overview

This is not a crash diet. It's a structured approach to body composition that respects growth, performance, and long-term health. The program adjusts based on the athlete's goal (gain, lose, or maintain) but the training structure is similar — heavy, progressive strength work that signals the body to keep (or build) muscle.

**Block structure:**

- Weeks 1–7: Training + nutrition in action
- Week 8: Deload + reassessment

**Goal-based calorie adjustments:**

- **Gaining:** 200–400 cal surplus
- **Losing:** 300–500 cal deficit
- **Maintaining:** at maintenance (est. TDEE)

## Who It Is For

- Athletes who want to add lean muscle without excessive fat gain
- Athletes who want to lose fat while preserving performance
- Anyone who wants to "recomp" (slowly swap fat for muscle)
- Athletes whose sport has weight classes — **with supervision**

**Not for:**
- Adolescents in a growth spurt who want to lose weight — get medical input first
- Anyone with a history of disordered eating
- Athletes in-season with high game volume

## Medical Note for Adolescents

High school athletes are still growing. Restrictive diets can impair growth, hormonal development, bone density, and performance. **Do not start a fat-loss phase without a coach, parent, and preferably a doctor or dietitian involved.**

For adolescent athletes:

- Prefer maintenance or slight surplus (recomp) over active cutting
- Target slow rates: 0.5% bodyweight per week max for loss
- Never drop below 30 kcal/kg fat-free mass per day (RED-S threshold)
- Protein is non-negotiable: 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day

## Weekly Structure

Four training days per week (upper/lower split) plus optional cardio:

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Upper A (heavy) |
| Tuesday | Lower A (heavy) |
| Thursday | Upper B (moderate) |
| Friday | Lower B (moderate) |
| 2–3× week | Optional cardio: 20–30 min LISS, or 10 min intervals |

## Sample Session — Upper A

1. **Bench Press** — 4×5–6 @ RPE 7
2. **Bent-Over Row** — 4×6–8 @ RPE 7
3. **Overhead Press** — 3×6–8 @ RPE 7
4. **Lat Pulldown** — 3×8–10 @ RPE 7
5. **Triceps + Bicep Superset** — 3×10–12 each

## Sample Session — Lower A

1. **Back Squat** — 4×5–6 @ RPE 7
2. **Romanian Deadlift** — 3×8–10 @ RPE 7
3. **Leg Press** — 3×10–12 @ RPE 7
4. **Leg Curl** — 3×10–12
5. **Calf Raise** — 4×12–15
6. **Core** — 3 rounds

## The Nutrition Framework

**Step 1 — Establish maintenance.**
Track everything you eat for 7–10 days. Weigh yourself daily. Average the week. If weight is stable, that's your maintenance.

**Step 2 — Set protein target.**
1.6–2.2 g/kg bodyweight per day. This is the anchor. Get this right first.

**Step 3 — Set calories.**
- Gain: maintenance + 200–400 cal
- Lose: maintenance − 300–500 cal
- Maintain: at maintenance

**Step 4 — Distribute the rest.**
- Fat: 0.8–1.0 g/kg per day minimum
- Carbs: fill the remainder (fuel for training)

**Step 5 — Track weekly average.**
Daily weight fluctuates. Track the 7-day average and adjust on 2-week trends, not daily numbers.

## Rate Targets

- **Gaining:** 0.25–0.5% bodyweight per week (150 lb athlete: 0.4–0.75 lb/week)
- **Losing:** 0.5–1% bodyweight per week (150 lb athlete: 0.75–1.5 lb/week)
- **Maintaining:** ±1% of target weight

If you're outside these ranges for 2 weeks, adjust calories by 100–200.

## Training Progression

Same as a strength program: add 5–10 lbs per week on main lifts while keeping RPE 7–8.

**Critical:** don't try to set PRs during a cut. You're trying to maintain strength, not build it. If a lift holds steady while you drop weight, you're winning.

## Tracking That Helps

**Daily:**
- Bodyweight (fasted, morning, post-bathroom)
- Calories, protein, carbs, fat
- Training session (lifts, RPE)

**Weekly:**
- Average weight
- Waist circumference
- Energy levels (1–10)
- Sleep quality (1–10)

**Every 4 weeks:**
- Progress photos (same lighting, same pose)
- Full measurements (chest, waist, arms, thighs, hips)
- Strength check (are main lifts holding?)

## Deload Protocol

**Week 8 (and every 8 weeks):**

- Cut training volume 40%
- Keep intensity (same weights)
- Return to maintenance calories for 1 week
- Reassess goals

If you've been in a cut for 8+ weeks, take a full diet break — 1–2 weeks at maintenance.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Going too fast.** Aggressive cuts cost muscle and hormones.
- **Skipping protein.** The single biggest lever.
- **Reacting to daily weight.** Trust the weekly average.
- **Cutting cardio-only.** You lose muscle without lifting.
- **Cutting during a growth spurt.** You will hurt your development.
- **Not sleeping.** Poor sleep shifts weight loss toward lean tissue.

## When to Switch

- **After 8 weeks:** reassess. Repeat, switch goals, or move to a performance-focused block.
- **If performance drops significantly:** stop the deficit, return to maintenance.
- **If you're a growing teenager and results are slow:** that's normal — your body is prioritizing development, not physique.

## Journal

Uses the **Training Log — Weight Management** template. Track daily weight, calories, protein, training, and weekly averages.
