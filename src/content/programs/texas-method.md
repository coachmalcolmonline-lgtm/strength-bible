# Texas Method

Texas Method is an intermediate strength program organized around three
training days per week, each with a distinct purpose: Volume, Recovery, and
Intensity. It is the natural successor to Starting Strength and StrongLifts.

## Overview

The program uses a weekly cycle rather than a session cycle. Volume Day
drives adaptation. Recovery Day accelerates it. Intensity Day tests it. Over
a week, the athlete accumulates fatigue, recovers from it, and then expresses
new strength on Intensity Day.

## Who It Is For

Texas Method is for athletes who have completed a linear progression program
(Starting Strength or StrongLifts), whose progression has stalled, and who
now need a weekly progression model instead of a session-to-session model.

## Prerequisites

1. Complete all foundational concepts.
2. Finish a linear progression program (or equivalent 3-6 months of training).
3. Know your working weights for all main lifts.
4. Understand the difference between Volume, Recovery, and Intensity work.

## The Three Days

**Volume Day (Monday)**
- Back Squat 5x5 at 90% of Intensity Day weight
- Bench Press 5x5 or Press 5x5
- Power Clean 5x3 or Deadlift 1x5

**Recovery Day (Wednesday)**
- Back Squat 2x5 at 80% of Volume Day weight
- Press 3x5 or Bench Press 3x5
- Accessory: pull-ups, back extensions, core

**Intensity Day (Friday)**
- Back Squat 1x5 (new PR attempt)
- Bench Press 1x5 (new PR attempt)
- Deadlift 1x5 (new PR attempt)

## Weekly Structure

| Day | Focus | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Volume Day | High volume, moderate load |
| Tuesday | Recovery / Mobility | Light walk, SMR |
| Wednesday | Recovery Day | Low volume, low load |
| Thursday | Recovery / Mobility | Light walk, SMR |
| Friday | Intensity Day | Low volume, max load |
| Saturday | Optional / Off | — |
| Sunday | Full Rest | — |

## Progression Rules

**Volume Day:** Add 5 lbs to squat/bench/press, 10 lbs to deadlift each week.

**Intensity Day:** Attempt new 5-rep maxes each week. If you hit them, progression is working. If you miss, reset by 10% and rebuild.

**Recovery Day:** Fixed percentage of Volume Day weight (80%). Do not push this day.

**When you stall on Intensity Day:**

1. First miss: repeat the same weight next week.
2. Second miss: drop Intensity Day weight by 10%, rebuild.
3. Third miss after reset: switch programs (Wendler 5/3/1 or PPL).

## Autoregulation in Practice

Texas Method is one of the harder programs to autoregulate — the structure
is tight. But:

- **Volume Day weight** can be adjusted if recovery is poor.
- **Intensity Day attempts** should not be forced if readiness is low.
- **Sleep, nutrition, and stress** drive 80% of Texas Method success.
- **If a full week goes badly**, run a lighter volume day and skip Intensity Day.

## Sample 4-Week Cycle

Assume Intensity Day squat at 315 lbs.

Week 1:
- Volume: 285x5x5
- Recovery: 225x2x5
- Intensity: 320x5 (PR attempt)

Week 2:
- Volume: 290x5x5
- Recovery: 230x2x5
- Intensity: 325x5

Week 3:
- Volume: 295x5x5
- Recovery: 235x2x5
- Intensity: 330x5

Week 4:
- Volume: 300x5x5
- Recovery: 240x2x5
- Intensity: 335x5

If Intensity Day PRs stop coming, reset.

## When to Switch Programs

Move on when:

1. Volume Day has become so heavy that recovery is impossible.
2. Intensity Day PRs have stalled after two resets.
3. You want more variety in programming.
4. You want to specialize (Smolov, Smolov Jr.).

## Common Pitfalls

- Pushing too hard on Recovery Day (it's a recovery day).
- Under-eating on weekends (Volume Day requires fuel).
- Skipping Volume Day and expecting Intensity Day to go well.
- Not sleeping enough.
- Running Texas Method too early (before finishing linear progression).

## Related Programs

- Starting Strength — predecessor.
- StrongLifts 5x5 — predecessor.
- Wendler 5/3/1 — monthly-cycle successor.
- PPL — hypertrophy-focused alternative.
