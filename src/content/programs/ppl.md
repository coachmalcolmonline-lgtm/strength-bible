# PPL (Push/Pull/Legs)

PPL is a 6-day hypertrophy split organized around three movement patterns:
Push, Pull, and Legs. Each pattern is trained twice per week. It is one of
the most popular and effective programs for building muscle mass while
maintaining strength.

## Overview

The week alternates Push, Pull, Legs, Push, Pull, Legs — then rest. Each
session focuses on a primary movement pattern (with 3-4 exercises), allowing
high volume per muscle group while giving each pattern 72 hours to recover.

## Who It Is For

PPL is for athletes who have completed a strength foundation program
(Starting Strength, StrongLifts, or the General Program), have at least 6
months of training experience, and now want to prioritize muscle growth
(hypertrophy) without abandoning strength work.

## Prerequisites

1. Complete all foundational concepts.
2. Master all barbell lifts and bodyweight movements.
3. Know your working weights.
4. Have 6+ months of consistent training experience.

## The Three Sessions

**Push Day**
- Bench Press 4x6-8 (primary)
- Overhead Press 3x8-10
- Incline Dumbbell Press 3x10-12
- Lateral Raises 3x12-15
- Triceps (dips or extensions) 3x10-15

**Pull Day**
- Deadlift or Rack Pull 4x5 (primary)
- Barbell Row 3x8-10
- Pull-ups or Lat Pulldowns 3x8-12
- Face Pulls 3x12-15
- Biceps (barbell or dumbbell curls) 3x10-12

**Legs Day**
- Back Squat 4x6-8 (primary)
- Romanian Deadlift 3x8-10
- Leg Press or Lunges 3x10-12
- Leg Curls 3x12-15
- Calves 3x15-20

## Weekly Structure

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Push |
| Tuesday | Pull |
| Wednesday | Legs |
| Thursday | Push |
| Friday | Pull |
| Saturday | Legs |
| Sunday | Full Rest |

Alternate variation: 3-day PPL (one rotation per week) if recovery is limited.

## Progression Rules

**Primary lifts:** Add 5 lbs when you hit the top of the rep range on all
sets at RPE 8 or below.

**Accessory lifts:** Add reps until top of range, then add weight.

**Volume target:** 10-20 hard sets per muscle group per week. PPL naturally
achieves this by hitting each muscle group twice weekly.

**Deloading:** Every 6-8 weeks, reduce volume by 40% and intensity by 20%
for one week.

## Autoregulation in Practice

PPL uses RPE 7-9 for most working sets:

- **RPE 7** — 3 reps left. Productive warm-up or volume.
- **RPE 8** — 2 reps left. Working intensity.
- **RPE 9** — 1 rep left. Near failure. Use sparingly.

If readiness is poor, reduce accessory volume first, not primary lifts.

## Sample 8-Week Cycle

Week 1-3: Establish baseline, RPE 7-8.
Week 4: Push intensity — primary lifts at RPE 8-9.
Week 5-6: Continue progression.
Week 7: Deload — reduce volume 40%, RPE 5-7.
Week 8: Resume at week 6 loads.

## When to Switch Programs

Move on when:

1. You want to prioritize strength instead of size.
2. You want to specialize on one lift.
3. You want more variety.

Options: Wendler 5/3/1, Texas Method, Smolov.

## Common Pitfalls

- Skipping Legs Day (it's the most important).
- Too many exercises per session (keep to 5-6).
- Not progressing primary lifts.
- Not eating enough to support muscle growth.
- Training to failure on every set.

## Related Programs

- Wendler 5/3/1 — strength-focused alternative.
- Texas Method — strength-focused alternative.
- Smolov — squat specialization.
- General Program — foundation.
