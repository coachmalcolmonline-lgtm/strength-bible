# Wendler 5/3/1

Wendler 5/3/1 is a monthly-cycle strength program built around four main
lifts: Back Squat, Bench Press, Deadlift, and Press. Every session is built
from a percentage of a Training Max — not your true 1RM — and every cycle
ends with an AMRAP set that drives progression.

## Overview

The program is organized in 4-week cycles:

- Week 1: 5/5/5+ (top set is 5+ reps)
- Week 2: 3/3/3+ (top set is 3+ reps)
- Week 3: 5/3/1+ (top set is 1+ reps)
- Week 4: Deload (light sets, no AMRAP)

All weights are percentages of your **Training Max**, which is 90% of your
true 1RM. Progression happens through the AMRAP set — the more reps you hit,
the more your Training Max rises next cycle.

## Who It Is For

Wendler 5/3/1 is for athletes who have trained consistently for at least six
months, have established 1RMs on all four main lifts, and want a sustainable
program they can run for years. It is one of the most tested intermediate
strength programs in the world.

## Prerequisites

1. Complete all foundational concepts.
2. Master all four main lifts.
3. Establish 1RMs (or estimate from recent AMRAP).
4. Know how to run a Training Max calculation (90% of 1RM).

## The Four Lifts

- Back Squat
- Bench Press
- Deadlift
- Press

## Weekly Structure

Wendler is flexible. The most common template is 4 days per week:

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Press |
| Tuesday | Deadlift |
| Thursday | Bench Press |
| Friday | Back Squat |

Alternative templates: 3 days, 2 days, or 6 days. The cycle structure stays
the same regardless.

## Cycle Structure

| Week | Set Structure | Percentages |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 3 sets: 5 / 5 / 5+ | 65% / 75% / 85% of TM |
| Week 2 | 3 sets: 3 / 3 / 3+ | 70% / 80% / 90% of TM |
| Week 3 | 3 sets: 5 / 3 / 1+ | 75% / 85% / 95% of TM |
| Week 4 | Deload: 3 sets of 5 at 40/50/60% | Recovery week |

The "+" means AMRAP — as many reps as possible at that weight.

## Progression Rules

**After each cycle**, recalculate your Training Max:

- Take the weight used on your top set.
- Multiply by reps performed.
- Multiply that by 0.0333.
- Add the top set weight to that number.
- The result is your new estimated 1RM.
- Multiply by 0.9 → that's your new Training Max.

Or simpler: **add 5 lbs to Press and Bench, 10 lbs to Squat and Deadlift**
after each successful cycle.

## Autoregulation in Practice

Wendler is highly structured but leaves room for adjustment:

- **On the AMRAP set**, push hard but stop 1 rep before technical failure.
- **On bad days**, aim for the minimum reps — progress still accumulates.
- **If a cycle fails** (miss reps on top set), drop Training Max by 10%
  and rebuild.

## Sample 4-Week Cycle

Assume Training Max of 300 lbs on Squat:

Week 1: 195x5, 225x5, 255x5+
Week 2: 210x3, 240x3, 270x3+
Week 3: 225x5, 255x3, 285x1+
Week 4: 120x5, 150x5, 180x5 (deload)

## Assistance Work

Wendler recommends "Boring But Big" (BBB) as the default assistance template:

- After the main lift, do 5x10 of the same lift at 50-60% of Training Max.
- Or 5x10 of the opposite lift.

Other templates: Triumvirate, Periodization Bible, Bodyweight.

## When to Switch Programs

Wendler is designed to be run for years. Consider switching when:

1. You want to specialize (Smolov, Smolov Jr.).
2. You want hypertrophy focus (PPL).
3. You want a different strength approach (Texas Method).

## Common Pitfalls

- Using true 1RM instead of Training Max (should be 90%).
- Going to failure on AMRAP.
- Skipping the deload week.
- Adding weight faster than the program prescribes.
- Doing too much assistance work.

## Related Programs

- Texas Method — weekly progression alternative.
- PPL — hypertrophy-focused alternative.
- Smolov — squat specialization.
- Starting Strength / StrongLifts — linear progression predecessors.
